## 1. POSITIONING

### What We Are
Regulatory operationalization infrastructure for Canadian mid-market regulated industries.

We convert regulatory change into operational action automatically — with human review at every step.

### What We Are Not
- An EHS platform (do not compete with Cority or Intelex on features)
- A compliance dashboard (do not lead with reporting or analytics)
- An AI tool (buyers don't buy AI; they buy outcomes)
- A legal service (we provide operational compliance support, not legal advice)

### Core Positioning Statement
> "When regulations change, your operations update automatically."

### Wedge Message (Year One)
> "Stop monitoring regulations manually. We watch every regulator in your jurisdiction, interpret what changed, and tell you exactly what to update — with draft language ready."

### What to Say to Different Buyers

| Buyer | What They Care About | How to Frame It |
|---|---|---|
| Safety Manager / HSE Director | Time savings, audit readiness, not missing changes | "We eliminate 15–20 hours/month of regulatory monitoring. You get a weekly digest telling you exactly what changed and what to do about it." |
| COO / VP Operations | Risk reduction, cost control | "One missed regulatory update can cost $50K+ in fines and audit remediation. We cost $3K/month and catch every change." |
| Legal / HR | Liability, AI risk | "Every output is reviewed by a qualified compliance professional before it reaches you. AI accelerates the work. A human stands behind every recommendation." |
| IT / Procurement | Integration, security | "Azure Canada Central hosting. Data residency in Canada. Integration with SharePoint and Teams. SOC 2 roadmap on request." |

## 2. IDEAL CUSTOMER PROFILE

### Primary ICP — Alberta Oil & Gas Contractor

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Industry | Oil & gas operators and contractors |
| Geography | Alberta (primary), Saskatchewan (secondary) |
| Size | 100–500 employees |
| Safety program maturity | Existing safety program; COR (Certificate of Recognition) holder |
| Current compliance approach | Manual monitoring + external safety consultant |
| Document management | SharePoint or network drive |
| Pain signal | Has experienced an audit or WCB inspection in the last 2 years |
| Budget indicator | Currently spending $1,500–$5,000/month on safety consulting |
| Decision makers | Safety Manager/HSE Director (champion) + COO/VP Operations (buyer) |

**Why this ICP first:**
- Highest regulatory burden in Canada (provincial OH&S + AER + federal + site-specific)
- High spend on safety consultants — budget already exists and allocated
- Dense contractor network — one reference sells the next
- Safety culture makes compliance failures real and urgent
- Founder has domain familiarity in this space

### Secondary ICP — Canadian Municipality

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Industry | Municipal government |
| Geography | Alberta and BC (initial), Ontario (expansion) |
| Size | 200–2,000 employees |
| Safety program maturity | Formal program; limited internal AI adoption |
| Document management | SharePoint |
| Pain signal | Multiple departments with different regulatory obligations; limited internal safety expertise |
| Decision makers | Director of Corporate Services or HR (champion) + CAO (buyer) |

**Why this ICP second:** Longer sales cycles (municipal procurement), but large ACV potential and strong reference value for other municipalities.

### Disqualifying Criteria (Do Not Pursue)
- Companies under 50 employees — insufficient budget and complexity to justify onboarding cost
- US-based companies — out of scope until Canadian market is proven
- Enterprises over 2,000 employees — sales cycles 12–18 months; will exhaust runway
- Companies using Cority or Intelex as primary EHS system — displacement sale; not year-one priority

## 3. SALES MOTION

### Model: Founder-Led, Relationship-Driven, Referral-Accelerated

No inbound SaaS motion in year one. Buyers in regulated industries do not search for AI compliance tools. You find them.

### Sales Stages

```
1. PROSPECT IDENTIFICATION
   └── Sources: Industry network, ACSA/CSSE member lists, LinkedIn,
                safety consultant referrals, conference contacts

2. OUTREACH
   └── Direct message or call to Safety Manager or HSE Director
   └── Lead with pain, not product: "How are you currently tracking
       regulatory changes from Alberta OHS and AER?"
   └── Do not pitch the AI platform — pitch the problem you solve

3. DISCOVERY CALL (30 min)
   └── Uncover: current regulatory monitoring process,
       time spent, audit history, consultant spend
   └── Qualify: budget exists, decision maker access, regulatory scope
   └── Goal: Get agreement that the problem is painful and costly

4. DEMO (45–60 min)
   └── Show: live regulatory digest, SOP gap alert, draft revision language
   └── Do NOT show: platform roadmap, future features, enterprise vision
   └── Use: a real Alberta OHS change from the last 90 days
   └── End with: "What would it mean to have this in your inbox every week?"

5. PAID PILOT (30–60 days)
   └── Scope: 1–2 regulatory bodies, client's existing SOP library
   └── Price: $1,500–$2,500 (not free — free pilots attract non-buyers)
   └── Deliverable: Weekly regulatory digest + 1 gap analysis on a real change
   └── Goal: Demonstrate time savings and accuracy. Get internal champion.

6. ANNUAL CONTRACT
   └── Convert pilot to annual contract at standard tier pricing
   └── Onboarding fee: $2,000–$5,000 (charged separately — covers SOP ingestion)
   └── Payment terms: Annual upfront or quarterly
   └── Contract includes: regulatory scope definition, SLA, data handling terms
```

### Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We already have a safety consultant for this." | "We make your consultant more effective. They stop spending 10 hours/month monitoring regulations and start spending that time on the work that needs their expertise. Some clients keep their consultant as our reviewer." |
| "What if the AI gets something wrong?" | "That's why every output is reviewed by a qualified compliance professional before it reaches you. You're not trusting AI. You're trusting a reviewed, sourced interpretation with a full audit trail." |
| "We're not ready for AI tools." | "You don't interact with the AI at all. You get a weekly email telling you what changed and what to update. Your workflow doesn't change — you just stop spending 15 hours a month monitoring websites." |
| "We need to run this through IT/procurement." | "Understood. We run on Azure Canada Central — your data stays in Canada. Happy to provide security documentation and answer IT's questions directly." |
| "The price is too high." | "You're currently spending [X] with your consultant for this work. We cost [Y]. That's [Z] in savings/month. We can start with a 30-day paid pilot at $1,500 so you can validate the value before committing." |

### Pricing Discipline
- Do not discount more than 20% for any customer
- Do not offer free pilots — free signals low value and attracts tire-kickers
- Onboarding fee is non-negotiable — it represents real labor and creates switching costs
- Customers 1–3: Accept 10–15% discount in exchange for testimonials, case study rights, and reference calls

## 4. CHANNEL STRATEGY

### Primary Channel: Direct Outreach
Founder-led direct outreach to Safety Managers and HSE Directors. This is the only reliable year-one channel. Do not delegate this.

**Tactics:**
- LinkedIn direct message — safety managers are active on LinkedIn
- Industry contact network — warm introductions are 10x more effective than cold
- Conference follow-up — targeted presence at OHS Canada, provincial safety summits

### Channel 1: Safety Consultant Partnerships (Highest Leverage)
Target 2–3 Alberta EHS consulting firms who do manual compliance monitoring work for mid-market industrial clients.

**Value proposition to them:** "We automate the monitoring and interpretation work. You focus on the advisory work that needs your expertise. You can serve 3x the clients with the same headcount. Revenue share or referral fee on clients you bring."

**Structure options:**
- Referral fee: 10–15% of first year contract value
- Co-delivery: Consultant handles human review; Vaquero handles AI + platform; revenue split
- White-label (defer until 20+ customers): Consultant brands the output

**Why this channel matters:** Consultants already have the relationships and the trust. You bypass 3–6 months of trust-building.

### Channel 2: Industry Associations
- **ACSA** (Alberta Construction Safety Association) — member access, conference presence
- **ENFORM** — oil & gas safety association, high-value network
- **CSSE** (Canadian Society of Safety Engineering) — national network, professional credibility
- **AASP** (Alberta Association of Safety Partnerships)

**Approach:** Seek speaking opportunities, sponsor relevant events, pursue member communication access. Budget $10,000–$20,000/year for association presence. Not for brand awareness — for direct relationship access.

### Channel 3: Insurance Brokers (18-Month Play)
Commercial insurance brokers (Marsh, AON, BFL) manage liability programs for industrial clients. If you can demonstrate that documented compliance programs reduce claims exposure, insurers become a distribution channel.

**Early action:** Establish one relationship with a mid-market industrial lines broker. Position as a risk reduction tool. Build the narrative that compliance documentation = insurance defensibility. This channel takes 12–18 months to activate but becomes powerful.

### Channel 4: Microsoft Partner Ecosystem
Teams/SharePoint integration is a credibility signal and distribution lever for municipal and enterprise clients.

**Action:** Register in Microsoft Partner Network. Pursue ISV designation when integration is production-ready (Phase 2). This opens Microsoft's enterprise customer introductions and adds procurement credibility.

### What NOT to Pursue (Year One)
- **Content marketing / SEO** — too slow. Regulated industry buyers don't search for new tools.
- **Paid digital advertising** — wrong channel for this buyer and purchase decision
- **Broad industry conferences** — expensive and diffuse. Only targeted association events.
- **Inbound SaaS motion** — requires brand and content authority you don't have yet

## 5. FIRST 10 CUSTOMERS STRATEGY

### Customers 1–3: Network Conversion
- Source: Founder's existing industry relationships
- Approach: Direct conversation, not cold outreach
- Pricing: 10–15% discount acceptable in exchange for: written testimonial, case study rights, reference call availability
- Goal: Prove the workflow works. Get documented time savings metrics. Get one quote you can use in outreach.

### Customers 4–6: Referral + Consultant Channel
- Source: Referrals from customers 1–3 + first consultant partner
- Pricing: Standard tier. No discounts.
- Goal: Prove repeatability. Second and third clients from different sources validates the motion.

### Customers 7–10: Direct Outreach With Proof
- Source: Cold LinkedIn outreach + association relationships, backed by case studies from customers 1–6
- Pricing: Standard tier. Full onboarding fee.
- Goal: Prove you can close without warm introductions. Prove the case study converts.

## 6. COMPETITIVE POSITIONING

### The Landscape

| Competitor | Threat Level | Why You Win |
|---|---|---|
| Cority | Low (different ICP) | They target 1,000+ employee enterprise. You own 50–500. Don't fight upmarket. |
| Intelex (Fortive) | Medium-High | Canadian roots, capital for AI. Watch for acquisition of regulatory intelligence startups. Win on: specificity, speed, and cost. |
| BIS Safety Software | Medium | Same ICP. But they're a forms/documents tool, not intelligence. You win on: AI interpretation, audit trail, regulatory specificity. |
| Workhub | Low | Training-focused. Adjacent, not direct. |
| SiteDocs (Procore) | Low now, Medium long-term | Construction-specific. If Procore adds AI compliance, construction vertical gets harder. |
| Manual consultant | Primary competition | Every deal is displaced from a consultant budget. That's your real competition. |

### Win Conditions Against Manual Consultants
- Speed: Regulatory alerts in 24 hours vs. monthly consultant check-ins
- Coverage: Every source, every cycle vs. consultant's available hours
- Cost: $3,000–$6,000/month vs. $5,000–$15,000/month consultant spend
- Audit trail: Complete, timestamped, immutable vs. email threads and Word docs
- Consistency: Same quality every cycle vs. consultant variability

### Moat Development Sequence

| Moat | Timeline | How to Build It |
|---|---|---|
| Proprietary Canadian regulatory change corpus | Building now (from Day 1) | Log every change, every interpretation, every outcome. 2 years = irreplaceable dataset. |
| Client SOP corpus + switching costs | Month 3–6 (as SOP ingestion launches) | Once SOP library is ingested and mapped, switching costs are extreme. |
| Industry-specific interpretation accuracy | Month 6–18 | Oil & gas interpretations improve with each reviewed output. Vertical lock-in grows. |
| Audit trail as system of record | Ongoing | Clients can't delete 2 years of compliance decisions. You become infrastructure. |
| Consultant + association partnerships | Month 3–12 | Channel lock-in. Competitors can't easily replicate your distribution relationships. |

## 7. EXPANSION ROADMAP

### Vertical Expansion (Post-Beachhead)
Execute only after oil & gas beachhead is proven (10+ clients, documented retention, repeatable sales motion).

1. **Municipal governments** — Alberta and BC first. Different regulatory stack (municipal bylaws + provincial OH&S) but same pain. Strong reference value within municipal networks.
2. **Construction and utilities** — Post-oil & gas. Different regulatory sources (Procore owns the workflow layer here; you own the intelligence layer).
3. **Mining** — High regulatory burden, high compliance cost, limited digital maturity in mid-market.

### Geographic Expansion
- BC and Saskatchewan before Ontario — smaller markets, lower competition, easier to dominate
- Ontario only after BC is proven — larger market but more complex regulatory environment and higher competition
- **US: Not before Year 3.** 50-state jurisdiction complexity will fracture engineering and operations. Prove Canada first.

### Product Expansion
- **API layer** (Year 2–3) — allow EHS platforms to embed Vaquero's regulatory intelligence. Becomes distribution, not competition.
- **Insurer data product** (Year 3+) — aggregate compliance documentation patterns become valuable to insurers for risk modeling.

## 8. MESSAGING — WHAT TO AVOID

These phrases actively hurt sales in this market. Do not use in outreach, demos, or marketing.

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|---|---|
| "AI-powered compliance" | "Automated regulatory monitoring with human review" |
| "Machine learning" | Don't use this at all |
| "Platform" | "Service" (managed service phase) |
| "Disrupting EHS" | Never say this to a buyer |
| "Replaces your safety consultant" | "Makes your safety consultant more effective" or "reduces your dependency on external consultants" |
| "Fully automated" | "Automated with expert human review" |
| "Real-time" (unless you can prove it) | "Weekly regulatory digest" |
| "Guaranteed compliance" | You cannot guarantee compliance. Do not say this. Ever. |
