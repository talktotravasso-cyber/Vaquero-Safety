## SECTION A — Red Seal Compulsory Designation by Province

### Framework

Red Seal (Interprovincial Standard) certification is **portable** across all jurisdictions. The key distinction is:
- **Compulsory trade:** Must hold a journeyperson certificate (or be a registered apprentice) to legally perform restricted activities. Stop-work authority if absent.
- **Voluntary (non-compulsory):** Red Seal recognition only; no statutory bar on uncertified workers.[^10]

Issuing bodies:
- **AB:** Alberta Apprenticeship and Industry Training (AIT), under Alberta Advanced Education — https://tradesecrets.alberta.ca
- **BC:** SkilledTradesBC (formerly ITA) — https://skilledtradesbc.ca
- **SK:** Saskatchewan Apprenticeship and Trade Certification Commission (SATCC) — https://saskapprenticeship.ca
- **MB:** Apprenticeship Manitoba, Manitoba Advanced Education, Skills and Immigration — https://www.gov.mb.ca/apprenticeship
- **ON:** Skilled Trades Ontario (STO), under the *Building Opportunities in the Skilled Trades Act, 2021* — https://www.skilledtradesontario.ca
- **QC:** Commission de la construction du Québec (CCQ) for construction trades; Ministère de l'Emploi et Solidarité sociale (MESS) for non-construction trades — https://www.ccq.org

## SECTION B — Trade Licences Separate from Red Seal

### B1. Electrician — Ontario

**Answer:** Yes. Two distinct credentials are required to practice electrical work in Ontario on construction projects:

1. **Certificate of Qualification (C of Q) / Red Seal** — issued by **Skilled Trades Ontario (STO)** under the *Building Opportunities in the Skilled Trades Act, 2021*. Required to legally work in the compulsory trade of "Electrician — Construction and Maintenance." Annual renewal required.
   - Public registry: https://www.skilledtradesontario.ca (compulsory trades)
   
2. **ECRA/ESA Electrical Contractor Licence** — issued by the **Electrical Safety Authority (ESA)** under the *Electricity Act, 1998* and O. Reg. 570/05. Required for any business performing electrical contracting work in Ontario. The Master Electrician licence (also issued by ESA) designates the qualified tradesperson responsible for the work.
   - **Annual renewal:** Yes. The ECRA/ESA contractor licence must be renewed annually.
   - **Contractor lookup:** https://findacontractor.esasafe.com
   - ESA website: https://esasafe.com

**Critical distinction:** An individual journeyperson STO certificate is not equivalent to an ECRA/ESA contractor licence. A journeyperson electrician working under a licensed electrical contractor does not individually require the ECRA/ESA contractor licence, but the contractor they work for must hold one. Both are required in the licensing chain.

**Source:** https://esasafe.com/contractor-licensing-master-electricians/ — confirmed current May 2026[^21]

**Confidence: 3**

### B2. Electrician — British Columbia

**Answer:** Yes. Two distinct credentials are required in BC:

1. **Certificate of Qualification (Red Seal endorsement)** — issued by **SkilledTradesBC** (Construction Electrician is a compulsory Skilled Trades Certification trade since December 1, 2023). Required to legally work in the trade.
   - SkilledTradesBC portal: https://skilledtradesbc.ca

2. **Electrical Contractor Licence / Field Safety Representative (FSR) Certificate** — issued by **Technical Safety BC (TSBC)** under the *Safety Standards Act* and *Electrical Safety Regulation* (B.C. Reg. 100/2004).
   - FSR certificate (individual journeyperson): renewed **every three years** with proof of continuing education.
   - Electrical contractor licence: renewed **annually**.
   - TSBC verification/lookup: https://www.technicalsafetybc.ca/technologies/electrical/electrical-licences
   - TSBC Online Services (certificate lookup): https://portal.technicalsafetybc.ca/login

**Source:** https://www.technicalsafetybc.ca/technologies/electrical/electrical-licences — confirmed current[^22][^23][^24]

### B3. Gas Fitter — Ontario (TSSA G1/G2/G3)

**Answer:** Confirmed. Ontario uses a tiered gas technician certification system issued by the **Technical Standards and Safety Authority (TSSA)** under the *Technical Standards and Safety Act, 2000* and O. Reg. 215/01 (*Fuel Industry Certificates*):

| Certificate | Scope | Renewal |
|---|---|---|
| G3 | Entry level; installation/service of gas appliances up to 400,000 BTUH under supervision | Annual |
| G2 | Unrestricted residential, commercial, industrial gas work except systems >400,000 BTUH | Annual |
| G1 | Unrestricted — all gas systems including systems >400,000 BTUH | Annual |

- **Annual renewal:** Yes. All TSSA licences must be renewed annually. TSSA states: *"All licences must be renewed annually with the Technical Standards and Safety Authority (TSSA) in order to be considered valid and legal."*[^25]
- **TSSA Licence Verification:** TSSA does **not** have a publicly searchable self-serve portal for individual gas technician certificate verification. Verification is performed by emailing `publicinformationservices@tssa.org` with the certificate number or the technician's full legal name, date of birth, and address. No fee applies.[^5]
- TSSA main portal: https://www.tssa.org
- TSSA Client Portal (for licensees): https://tssa.org/contractor-registration-and-certification[^26]

**CFTA Gasfitter Reconciliation Agreement and Ontario:**

The CFTA Reconciliation Agreement to Improve Interjurisdictional Commerce and the Mobility of Gasfitters was finalized and published by the RCT in February 2026 (based on November 2025 negotiations). Key effects on Ontario:[^9][^8]
- It does **not** remove the TSSA G1/G2/G3 certification requirement for Ontario.
- It **establishes a timeline commitment** for Ontario (and other parties) to review and respond to completed incoming out-of-province gasfitter applications "expeditiously."
- It commits parties to incorporate common fuel safety standards (CSA B149.1) by reference in a timely and aligned fashion to reduce scope-of-work differences between jurisdictions.
- **Practical effect:** An incoming out-of-province gasfitter in good standing may apply to TSSA for Ontario certification, and Ontario is committed to processing the application within the agreed timeline. This streamlines labour mobility but does **not** grant automatic cross-provincial practice rights.

**Source:** https://rct-tccr.ca (Reconciliation Agreement PDF, February 2026) — Confidence: 3[^9]

### B4. Gas Fitter — British Columbia (TSBC Class A/B)

**Answer:** As of January 1, 2025, BC Class A and Class B gas fitting involves a **dual certification requirement**:

1. **SkilledTradesBC Certificate of Qualification with Red Seal Endorsement** — mandatory for new applicants under the *Skilled Trades BC Act*. Class A Gas Fitter became a compulsory Red Seal trade in BC in 2023; Class B joined on January 1, 2025.[^4][^3]

2. **Technical Safety BC (TSBC) Certificate of Qualification** — obtained by passing the TSBC Acts, Regulations, and Directives (ARD) exam. This is required separately from the SkilledTradesBC credential.
   - Gas certificates are **not** renewed on a fixed schedule in BC — the certificate closes three years after expiry if not renewed. The gas contractor licence under TSBC is renewed **annually**.[^27]
   - TSBC Gas Certification page: https://www.technicalsafetybc.ca/certification/gas[^27]
   - TSBC gas licence (contractor): renewed annually — https://www.technicalsafetybc.ca/technologies/gas/gas-licences[^28]

**Portability to Alberta:** Gas fitter certificates are **not generally portable** between provinces except under the Red Seal pathway or the CFTA Reconciliation Agreement. TSBC states: *"No other gas certificates are transferable between provinces."* For Alberta, an incoming BC gas fitter holding a Red Seal Gas Fitter A or B certificate from SkilledTradesBC may apply for Alberta AIT recognition under the Recognized Credentials Program.[^29]

**Source:** https://www.technicalsafetybc.ca/technologies/gas/gas-certification/transfer-to-bc — confirmed current — Confidence: 3[^29]

### B5. Gas Fitter — Alberta

**Answer:** Gasfitter Class A and Class B are **compulsory designated trades** in Alberta under the *Apprenticeship and Industry Training Act* (R.S.A. 2000, c. A-42.5).[^30][^11]

- **Issuing body:** Alberta Apprenticeship and Industry Training (AIT)
- **Certificate:** AIT Journeyperson Certificate (Gasfitter Class A or B). Red Seal endorsement available.
- **Separate annual licence beyond AIT certificate:** No separate annual operating licence is required in Alberta for a gasfitter (unlike Ontario's TSSA annual renewal model). The AIT Journeyperson Certificate itself is the credential. However, employers must ensure gas work is performed under proper permit regimes administered under the *Safety Codes Act* by accredited safety codes officers.
- **Credential verification:** https://tradesecrets.alberta.ca/trades-in-alberta/check-credentials/[^31]

**Source:** https://tradesecrets.alberta.ca/trades-in-alberta/profiles/0083/ (Gasfitter — Class A) — Confidence: 3[^30]

### B6. Gas Fitter — Quebec (RBQ)

**Answer:** In Quebec, gas installation work falls under the jurisdiction of the **Régie du bâtiment du Québec (RBQ)** under the *Act Respecting Building* (R-20.1) and applicable gas regulations.

- Gas fitting contractor licences are issued by the **RBQ** under subclasses 15.1–15.5 (plumbing, heating, oil burners, natural gas burners).
- **Qualification evaluations** for contractors specializing in plumbing, heating, or gas burners are conducted by the **Corporation des maîtres mécaniciens en tuyauterie du Québec (CMMTQ)**, which evaluates members' qualifications for RBQ licensing in these subclasses.[^32]
- **Annual renewal:** Yes. RBQ licences require annual maintenance fees. RBQ states: *"Every year, you must pay your licence in order to maintain it."*[^33]
- **RBQ licence registry (public):** https://www.pes.rbq.gouv.qc.ca/RegistreLicences (French only)[^34]
- **RBQ main site:** https://www.rbq.gouv.qc.ca/en/[^35]

**Portability to other provinces:** No. Quebec RBQ gas installation licences are jurisdiction-specific. Out-of-province gas fitters seeking to work in Quebec must apply through the CCQ for competency certification and, for gas work, through the RBQ/CMMTQ process. Cross-provincial recognition is addressed under the CFTA Reconciliation Agreement but does not grant automatic portability.[^9]

**Confidence: 3**

## SECTION D — COR Programs for Construction

### COR Framework

COR® (Certificate of Recognition) is nationally registered by the **Canadian Federation of Construction Safety Associations (CFCSA)**. Each province/territory has an **Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ)** — the certifying partner that grants COR® in that jurisdiction. COR® granted in one jurisdiction is **not automatically portable** to another jurisdiction; companies working across provinces must obtain COR® separately in each, although a CFCSA reciprocity letter process is available.[^43][^44]

**Standard audit cycle:** External audit (year 1) → Internal/maintenance audit (year 2) → Internal/maintenance audit (year 3) → External audit again. Minimum passing score: 80% overall with 50% in each element (varies slightly by province).[^45][^46]

## SECTION E — Ontario-Specific Compliance Details

### E1. Skilled Trades Ontario (STO) — Post-OCOT Structure

**Current status:** Skilled Trades Ontario is the successor body to the Ontario College of Trades (OCOT), which was dissolved under the *Building Opportunities in the Skilled Trades Act, 2021* (BOSTA). STO became fully operational with all OCOT functions transferred by 2022.

**STO mandate:**
- Registers apprentices and maintains training agreements
- Issues Certificates of Apprenticeship (C of A)
- Issues Certificates of Qualification (C of Q) to journeypersons in compulsory trades
- Maintains the **Public Register** of persons authorized to work in compulsory trades
- Enforces compulsory trade requirements (with MLITSD inspectors having stop-work authority)

**Compulsory trades currently registered through STO (23 trades):**

### E2. Ontario Working at Heights (WAH) Training

**Current status (confirmed May 2026):**

- **Requirement basis:** Ontario Regulation 297/13 (*Occupational Health and Safety Awareness and Training*) under the *Occupational Health and Safety Act* (OHSA). Workers on construction projects who use fall protection must complete CPO-approved WAH training before performing such work.
- **Renewal period:** **3 years** from date of completion. This has not changed post-2024. Workers must complete an approved WAH refresher course before expiry every 3 years.[^66][^67]
- **Approved by:** Chief Prevention Officer (CPO) of Ontario, under MLITSD. Provider approval is Ontario-specific and **not nationally portable**; a CPO-approved WAH certificate from an Ontario-approved provider is valid for Ontario construction projects only.
- **Refresher:** 4-hour in-person refresher (practical component required; cannot be completed entirely online).[^68]
- **Training record system:** As of February 26, 2024, all WAH training records migrated from SkillsPass to MLITSD's new Client Management System (CMS). Records accessible by calling MLITSD at 1-877-202-0008 or emailing WAH.Inquiryline@Ontario.ca.[^69]

### E3. WHMIS for Construction — Ontario vs. Federal

**Answer:** WHMIS in Ontario operates under a **coordinated federal-provincial framework**:

**Federal level:** The *Hazardous Products Act* (HPA) and *Hazardous Products Regulations* (HPR) govern the classification, labelling, and SDS requirements for suppliers. Health Canada is the federal regulator for supplier obligations. The HPR was amended in 2022, with suppliers given a 3-year transition period to comply (deadline: December 14, 2025). These amendments align Canada with GHS revision 7.[^73][^74][^75]

**Ontario level:** The *Occupational Health and Safety Act* (OHSA) and O. Reg. 860 (*Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System*) govern employer and worker WHMIS obligations in Ontario workplaces, including construction sites. The MLITSD enforces employer obligations (worker training, SDS access, labelling at the workplace level). There is no separate Ontario-specific WHMIS construction regulation beyond these; construction sites are covered by the general WHMIS provisions under OHSA.

**MLITSD enforcement activity:** MLITSD has been running a focused inspection campaign targeting employer WHMIS compliance with the 2022 HPR amendments, specifically updated worker training incorporating the new GHS 7 categories. This campaign is active and relevant to construction site employers.[^73]

**Key distinction for audit evidence:**
- Supplier SDS/label obligations → Federal (HPR, enforced by Health Canada)
- Employer worker training and workplace label obligations → Ontario (OHSA/O. Reg. 860, enforced by MLITSD)
- Both apply simultaneously on Ontario construction sites.

**Source:** https://whmis.org; https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/environmental-workplace-health/occupational-health-safety/workplace-hazardous-materials-information-system.html; https://www.wsps.ca/resource-hub/chemicals-hazardous-materials-whmis/new-hazardous-products-reg-amendments — confirmed current 2025 — Confidence: 3[^76][^74][^75][^73]
