### 1. ALBERTA (AB)

#### (a) Primary OHS Authority
**Alberta Occupational Health and Safety** (OHS), a division of the Government of Alberta, Alberta Labour and Immigration [Confidence: 3][^4]

#### (b) Enabling Legislation
**Occupational Health and Safety Act**, SA 2017, c. O-2.1 (in effect December 1, 2021 under the revised numbering)  
- Most recent OHS Code amendments: **Alberta Regulation 202/2024** (in force December 4, 2024) and **Alberta Regulation 210/2024** (in force December 10, 2024)[^5]
- OHS Act official URL: https://search-ohs-laws.alberta.ca/legislation/occupational-health-and-safety-act/[^6]

#### (c) Construction-Specific Regulation
**There is no standalone "construction regulation" in Alberta.** Construction work is governed by relevant Parts of the **Occupational Health and Safety Code**, Alberta Regulation 191/2021, consolidated to **210/2024 (current as of December 10, 2024)**[^7][^5]

The OHS Code is the single comprehensive regulation under the OHS Act. The following Parts apply specifically to construction-type work [Confidence: 3]:

| Part | Title | Relevance to Construction |
|------|-------|--------------------------|
| Part 6 | Cranes, Hoists and Lifting Devices | Tower cranes, mobile cranes, man-baskets, hoisting operations [^8] |
| Part 9 | Fall Protection | Work at heights ≥ 3 m; also < 3 m with unusual injury risk; travel restraint, fall arrest systems [^1] |
| Part 23 | Scaffolds and Temporary Work Platforms | Single/double pole scaffolds, swingstage scaffolds, suspended scaffolds [^9] |
| Part 30 | Demolition | Structural demolition procedures [^10] |
| Part 32 | Excavating and Tunnelling | Ground disturbance, soil classification, shoring, trenching [^11] |
| Part 4 | Chemical Hazards, Biological Hazards and Harmful Substances | Asbestos (Schedule 1, Table 1); designated substances at construction sites [^12][^13] |

### 6. QUÉBEC (QC)

#### (a) Primary OHS Authority
**Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail (CNESST)** — primary OHS enforcement and prevention body for Quebec[^47][^48]

**Commission de la construction du Québec (CCQ)** — governs labour relations, workforce placement, and compliance with the Act Respecting Labour Relations, Vocational Training and Workforce Management in the Construction Industry (Act R-20)[^49][^50]

#### (b) Enabling Legislation
**Act Respecting Occupational Health and Safety (Loi sur la santé et la sécurité du travail — LSST)**, RLRQ, c. S-2.1  
- Major reform: **Bill 59 (An Act to Modernize the Occupational Health and Safety Regime)** amendments, final phase in force **October 1, 2025** (Order in Council 1154-2025)[^48]
- This overhaul mandated prevention programs (≥20 workers) or action plans (<20 workers), health and safety committees, contaminant registers, and expanded CNESST oversight[^48]

#### (c) Construction-Specific Regulation
**Safety Code for the Construction Industry (Code de sécurité pour les travaux de construction)**, RLRQ, c. S-2.1, r.4 [Confidence: 3][^51]

This is the primary construction-specific safety regulation under the LSST. It is current and remains in force as the primary technical safety standard for construction worksites in Quebec.

#### (d) Official URL
- Official Légis Québec text of S-2.1, r.4: https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/S-2.1,%20r.4

> **Research note:** The direct URL above is the standard Légis Québec path for the current official text; the regulation citation S-2.1, r.4 is confirmed by multiple sources.[^52][^51]

#### (e) Post-2022 Amendments
- **January 1, 2024**: New mandatory training and certification requirements for construction site committee members, part-time representatives, full-time health and safety officers, and OHS coordinators[^53]
- **April 2024**: Amendments to S-2.1, r.4 regarding worker rescue following a fall (Council of Ministers filing)[^52]
- **October 1, 2025**: Bill 59 provisions in force — mandatory prevention programs, health and safety committees, contaminant registers[^48]
- **April 17, 2025**: Québec adopted NBC 2020 building code with provincial variations[^54]

#### CCQ vs. CNESST Roles on Construction Sites

Both the CCQ and the CNESST **are active on the same construction worksite**, but with distinct and non-overlapping mandates [Confidence: 3]:[^50][^49]

| Body | Mandate on Construction Sites |
|------|-------------------------------|
| **CNESST** | Enforces the LSST and the Safety Code for the Construction Industry (S-2.1, r.4). Investigates accidents, issues orders, conducts inspections, and enforces OHS compliance. Prevention and OHS enforcement authority. [^48][^55] |
| **CCQ** | Governs labour relations under Act R-20: worker placement, union referrals, apprenticeship programs, vocational training, payroll contributions, and verification that workers hold the correct trade classifications (journeyman/apprentice). Does not enforce OHS — that role belongs exclusively to CNESST. [^49][^56] |

Both can be present simultaneously. A CCQ inspector may be on site verifying worker trade credentials while a CNESST inspector verifies fall protection compliance — these are separate inspections under separate statutory authorities. [Confidence: 3][^49]

### QUESTION 1: ONTARIO — O. Reg. 213/91 Status, January 2025 Amendment, and URL

**Status:** O. Reg. 213/91 — Construction Projects is **confirmed in force** as of May 2026. [Confidence: 3][^41]

**Current consolidation period:** From April 20, 2026 to the e-Laws currency date.[^41]

**Last amendment:** O. Reg. 116/26[^41]

**January 1, 2025 Amendments** included the following under O. Reg. 105/25 and related amending regulations:[^40][^39]
1. **Mandatory Defibrillators (AEDs):** Projects that regularly employ 20 or more workers for a period of 3 months or longer are required to have a Health Canada–licensed defibrillator (AED) readily available on site. This was further codified in **O. Reg. 157/25** adding section 27.1 to O. Reg. 213/91, effective January 1, 2026.[^46][^40]
2. **Washroom Facility Recordkeeping:** Constructors must maintain a record of washroom servicing (all cleaning and sanitizing activities) for the previous six months or for the duration of the project, whichever is shorter.[^40]

### QUESTION 2: QUÉBEC — Safety Code S-2.1, r.4 Status; CCQ vs. CNESST Roles

**Current Status of S-2.1, r.4:** The *Safety Code for the Construction Industry* (Code de sécurité pour les travaux de construction), RLRQ, c. S-2.1, r.4, is the **current primary construction safety regulation** under the LSST in Québec. It is in force and was amended in April 2024 (requirements around rescue of a fallen worker). [Confidence: 3][^51][^52]

**URL:** https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/S-2.1,%20r.4

**CCQ vs. CNESST Roles — Confirmed Dual Authority:**

Both bodies are active on the same construction worksite with distinct, non-overlapping mandates [Confidence: 3]:

**CNESST** (Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail):
- Enforces the LSST (S-2.1) and the Safety Code for the Construction Industry (S-2.1, r.4)
- Responsible for: accident investigation, OHS inspections, issuing correction orders, prevention programs, training certification for OHS roles[^55][^48]
- January 2024: New mandatory training requirements for construction health and safety roles (committee members 1 hour; part-time representatives 3 hours; full-time representatives 40 hours; coordinators 240 hours)[^53]
- October 1, 2025: Prevention programs and action plans now mandatory under Bill 59 reform (Order in Council 1154-2025)[^48]
- URL: https://www.cnesst.gouv.qc.ca/

**CCQ** (Commission de la construction du Québec):
- Governs workforce management under the *Act Respecting Labour Relations, Vocational Training and Workforce Management in the Construction Industry* (Act R-20)
- Responsible for: trade classification verification, union referral, apprenticeship and journeyman status, payroll/levy collection, vocational training administration
- **Does not enforce OHS requirements** — that is the exclusive domain of CNESST[^56][^49]
- URL: https://www.ccq.org/[^56]

**Answer:** Yes, both authorities are active on the same worksite. A CCQ inspector may be verifying trade credentials at the same time a CNESST inspector is enforcing fall protection requirements. These are separate inspections under separate statutes. [Confidence: 3]

### QUESTION 7: ASBESTOS REGULATIONS — AB, BC, ON

#### ALBERTA — Asbestos on Construction Sites

**Governing regulation:** **Occupational Health and Safety Code**, Alberta Regulation 191/2021, **Part 4 — Chemical Hazards, Biological Hazards and Harmful Substances** (Schedule 1, Table 1 lists asbestos as a designated substance)[^13][^12]

**Threshold:** Alberta uses "any detectable" concentration as the threshold for asbestos-containing materials (ACM), the most stringent in Canada[^19]

**Key requirements:**
- Workers must receive asbestos training from a government-approved agency before handling asbestos in a restricted area[^91]
- 72-hour advance notification to Alberta OHS required before commencing any work that may release asbestos fibres (demolition, renovation, removal)[^91]
- The **Alberta Asbestos Abatement Manual** (updated March 2026) provides best practices guidance: https://www.alberta.ca/alberta-asbestos-abatement-manual[^91]

**URL (Part 4):** https://search-ohs-laws.alberta.ca/legislation/occupational-health-and-safety-code/part-4-chemical-hazards-biological-hazards-and-harmful-substances/[^12]

Alberta does not currently have a separate "Type 1/2/3" classification system as Ontario uses; instead, work is classified by risk level and restricted area designation under the OHS Code. [Confidence: 2]

#### BRITISH COLUMBIA — Asbestos on Construction Sites

**Governing regulation:** **OHS Regulation**, B.C. Reg. 296/97, **Part 6 — Substance-Specific Requirements**, specifically **Part 6.2 — Asbestos**[^24][^19]

**ACM threshold:** 0.5% asbestos by dry weight (general materials); any detectable for vermiculite[^19]

**January 1, 2024 Changes (Major):**
- **Workers:** Any person performing asbestos abatement work in relation to a building must be **certified** by completing training from a WorkSafeBC-approved provider[^26][^23]
- **Employers:** Any employer conducting asbestos abatement work for others must hold a **WorkSafeBC Asbestos Abatement Licence (AAL)**[^26][^23]
- BC is the **first province in Canada** to implement a formal licensing program for asbestos abatement contractors[^92]
- Certificates valid for 3 years; licences valid for 1 year (renewable annually)[^93]

**URLs:**
- Asbestos training, certification and licensing portal: https://www.worksafebc.com/en/health-safety/education-training-certification/asbestos-training-certification-licensing[^23]
- OHS Guidelines Part 6.2 (Asbestos): https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-guidelines/guidelines-part-06[^24]

BC does not use the Ontario "Type 1/2/3" classification system; it uses a risk-based approach under Part 6.2. [Confidence: 2]

#### ONTARIO — Asbestos on Construction Sites

**Governing regulation:** **O. Reg. 278/05 — Designated Substance — Asbestos on Construction Projects and in Buildings and Repair Operations**, under the Occupational Health and Safety Act [Confidence: 3][^43]

**Confirmed in force:** Yes. O. Reg. 278/05 is the current governing asbestos regulation for construction in Ontario. [Confidence: 3]

**Current consolidation period:** From April 20, 2026 to the e-Laws currency date[^43]
**Last amendment:** **O. Reg. 115/26** (made April 16, 2026; filed April 20, 2026; most provisions in force **July 1, 2026**)[^94]

**What O. Reg. 115/26 adds (effective July 1, 2026):**
- Adds definitions for "CSA" (CSA Group) to subsection 1(1)
- Adds definition for "NIOSH" (National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health)
- Amends paragraph 12 of section 14 (Type 1 operations) and paragraph 11 of section 15 (Type 2/3 operations) to add "or CSA" after "NIOSH" — allowing CSA-approved respirators in addition to NIOSH-approved respirators[^94]

**Type 1, Type 2, Type 3 Classification (as defined in O. Reg. 278/05, s. 12):** [Confidence: 3][^43]

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Type 1** | Low-risk operations: installing/removing ceiling tiles <7.5 m² without damage; removing non-friable ACM without cutting; hand-tool work on non-friable ACM with wetting; removing <1 m² drywall with ACM joint compound [^43] |
| **Type 2** | Medium-risk operations: accessing false ceilings over ACM; removal/disturbance of ≤1 m² friable ACM; enclosing friable ACM; applying tape/sealant to pipe insulation; installing/removing ≥7.5 m² ACM ceiling tiles without damage; power tool work on non-friable ACM with HEPA-equipped dust collection; glove bag pipe insulation removal; >1 m² drywall with ACM joint compound; filter cleaning in buildings with sprayed ACM fireproofing [^43] |

**ACM threshold in Ontario:** 0.5% asbestos by dry weight[^43]

### QUESTION 8: SILICA — Federal/Provincial OEL; Post-2022 Regulations

#### Ontario OEL for Crystalline Silica (Construction)

**Applicable regulation:** **O. Reg. 490/09 — Designated Substances** (applies to non-construction employers); silica is a designated substance[^44]

**OELs under O. Reg. 490/09:**
- **Quartz and tripoli:** 0.10 mg/m³ (8-hour TWA)
- **Cristobalite:** 0.05 mg/m³ (8-hour TWA)[^44]

> **Critical note:** O. Reg. 490/09 and its OEL for silica do **not** directly apply to employers on construction projects under O. Reg. 213/91. However, employers on construction projects still have a duty under section 25(2)(h) of the OHSA to "take every precaution reasonable in the circumstances for the protection of a worker," and section 30 of the OHSA requires project owners to determine if silica is present and inform contractors at the bidding stage. [Confidence: 3][^44]

**Silica OEL source (ON):** https://www.ontario.ca/document/silica-construction-projects/legal-requirements[^44]

#### Federal OEL for Crystalline Silica

**Applicable regulation:** **Canada Occupational Health and Safety Regulations**, SOR/86-304 (current to 2026-03-17; last amended 2025-03-26)[^95]

Federal OHS regulations apply to federally regulated workplaces (banking, telecommunications, inter-provincial transport, etc.) — **not to provincial construction projects.**

**Proposed federal amendments (October 2023):** The Canada Gazette, Part I, October 28, 2023, published proposed *Regulations Amending Certain Regulations Made Under the Canada Labour Code* which would establish new requirements for nanomaterials, thermal stress, non-solar UV radiation, and radon, and update references to incorporated standards — but these do **not** include new silica-specific construction requirements. [Confidence: 3][^96]

**Finding:** No Health Canada or ESDC federal silica-specific construction regulation has been enacted or formally proposed since 2022 as of the date of this research. The federal approach relies on existing OELs within the Canada OHS Regulations and ACGIH TLVs incorporated by reference. [Confidence: 2][^95][^96]

**Source URL (Canada OHS Regulations):** https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/sor-86-304/index.html[^95]

#### Provincial Silica OELs Applicable to Construction

Each province sets its own OEL for crystalline silica, typically in the OHS Code or equivalent regulation's chemical hazard tables. For audit purposes, specific OEL values for each province should be verified directly in the current chemical hazards schedule of the applicable OHS Code or Regulation.
