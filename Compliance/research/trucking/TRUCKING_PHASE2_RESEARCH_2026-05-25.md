## SECTION B — AIR BRAKE ENDORSEMENT: PORTABILITY

***

### B — Air Brake Endorsement by Province

The air brake endorsement is **not nationally portable** in a single-step mutual recognition sense. Each province issues its own endorsement on the driver's licence, and there is no single pan-Canadian endorsement. However, when a driver **transfers their licence** from one province to another, the receiving province will normally credit the out-of-province air brake endorsement **without requiring a full retest**, as all provinces follow the CCMTA standard. The critical point: **the endorsement must appear on the driver's licence issued by the current province of residence** — an out-of-province endorsement on a foreign-issued licence is not a standalone authority to operate air brakes in a new province.

| Province | Endorsement Name / Code | Required for Air-Brake Vehicles? | Transfer Credit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AB** | Code **Q** | Yes (all classes except Class 1, which includes it automatically) | Yes — credited on AB licence transfer[^3] |
| **BC** | Code **15** | Yes — for Class 5/7N drivers; integrated into Class 1/2/3 road test | Out-of-province endorsements credited on ICBC transfer, but a separate Code 15 or integrated qualification must appear on BC licence[^10][^11] |
| **SK** | Air Brake endorsement (separate SGI notation) | Yes — required for non-Class 1 drivers operating air-brake vehicles[^3] |  Credited on transfer[^3] |
| **MB** | Code **A** (authorized) | Yes — Classes 3, 4, 5; Class 1 drivers who have not tested on air brakes have Code **N** (not authorized)[^3] | Credited on transfer |
| **ON** | **Z endorsement** | Yes — required for *any* vehicle equipped with air brakes, including all commercial trucks, buses, large RVs[^21][^22] | Credited on transfer; however, ON now requires the **Air Brake Learning Module** (online, ~30 min) before Z endorsement renewal as of September 1, 2023[^20] |

## SECTION C — HOURS OF SERVICE TRAINING

***

### C1 — Nationally Standardized HOS Training Certificate

**Answer:** There is **no nationally standardized HOS training certificate** in Canada issued by a government body. HOS training is **employer-provided** and employer-certified. Transport Canada does not administer a national HOS training credential.[^33]

The CCMTA Application Guide (January 2007) for HOS regulations is the primary interpretive guidance document, but it does not constitute a training certificate program.[^33]

**Provincial legislation:** No Canadian province has legislated a specific HOS training program with a government-issued certification requirement as of May 2026. Carriers are required to ensure drivers are trained, but the training program design and documentation is the carrier's responsibility.[^33]

***

### C2 — ELD Operation Training

**Answer:** ELD operation training is **not a separate certification**. It is included within, or supplementary to, carrier-mandated HOS training. Transport Canada's ELD program requires certified ELDs to include an *Instructions for Use* document and carrier-accessible support, but does not mandate a separate training certificate for ELD operation.[^34][^35]

## SECTION D — TDG TRAINING CERTIFICATES

***

### D1 — TDG Training Renewal Period (Part 6)

**Answer:** Confirmed current. Under **SOR/2001-286, Part 6 (Training)**, TDG training certificates expire **3 years** after the date of issuance for road transport (and all modes except air, where the Part 6 schedule applies separately).[^36]

From **s. 6.3(1)(c)** of SOR/2001-286 (consolidated current to 2026-03-17): the certificate must include "the date the training certificate expires, preceded by the words 'Expires on' or 'Date d'expiration.'" The standard period is **3 years**.[^36]

The December 11, 2021 *Canada Gazette, Part I* proposed amendments to Part 6 training requirements (industry was notified of proposed changes to training record formats), but no final SOR implementing new training validity periods has been registered as of May 2026.[^37]

**Source:** SOR/2001-286, s. 6.3: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/sor-2001-286/section-6.3.html[^36]
**Confirmed current to:** March 17, 2026[^38]

***

### D2 — TDG Training Certificate Format

**Answer:** The TDG training certificate format is **employer-issued**, not nationally standardized. The certificate must include the specific items listed in **s. 6.3(1)** of SOR/2001-286: employer name and business address, employee name, expiry date, and the specific aspects of DG handling for which the employee is trained.[^36]

There is **no government-issued TDG training certificate template** — the certificate is issued by the employer or, for self-employed persons, by the self-employed person.[^36]

CCOHS (Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety) offers TDG training courses and information at: https://www.ccohs.ca/products/courses/tdg/[^39]

***

### D3 — Province-Specific TDG Training Requirements

**Answer:** No Canadian province has enacted TDG training requirements **beyond** the federal standard under SOR/2001-286. TDG is a federal statute; the *Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act, 1992* (TDG Act) is federal and applies uniformly across all provinces and territories. Provincial OHS requirements may require employers to document that TDG-trained employees have also received site-specific hazard training under provincial OHS legislation, but this is a general OHS duty, not a province-specific TDG certification.[^38]

**CCOHS TDG Information URL:** https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/chemicals/tdg.html[^39]

## SECTION E — LOAD SECUREMENT TRAINING

***

### E1 — Nationally Standardized Load Securement Certificate

**Answer:** There is **no nationally standardized, government-issued cargo securement training certificate** in Canada. NSC Standard 10 (*Cargo Securement*) establishes the performance standards for cargo securement systems, but does **not** establish a training certification requirement.[^40]

Load securement training is employer-provided under the general duty clause of each province's OHS legislation (employers must ensure workers are competent in their work tasks). The training standard referenced by most carriers and driver trainers is the CCMTA NSC Standard 10 content, but no certificate examination or issuing body is specified federally or provincially.

***

### E2 — Province-Specific Load Securement Training Requirements

**Answer:** No Canadian province has enacted a specific mandatory load securement training *certificate* requirement separate from the general competency standard under OHS legislation. Alberta's *OHS Act* and Code require workers to be competent, which implies training in cargo securement for workers performing that task, but no separate certificate is required.[^41]

***

### E3 — Flatdeck and Oversized Loads: AB and BC

**Answer:** Neither Alberta nor BC has enacted a legislated **cargo securement training certification** specifically for flatdeck or oversized-load operations. The CVSA Level I inspection verifies compliance with NSC Standard 10 on-road; no pre-employment credential is required.[^42][^40]

Industry programs (e.g., AMTA, SafetyDriven TSCBC) offer cargo securement training courses as part of their driver training programs, but these are voluntary industry programs, not legislated credentials.

> **Confidence Rating: 3 — Verified.** No federally or provincially mandated cargo securement certification exists.

## SECTION F — DANGEROUS GOODS DRIVER CERTIFICATION

***

### F1–F3 — Class 1 Placard Loads: Additional DG Driver Certification?

**Answer:** Beyond the standard **TDG training certificate** (SOR/2001-286, Part 6), there is **no additional federally legislated "Dangerous Goods Driver" certification** required for drivers transporting placard-required quantities.

The regulatory requirements for Class 1 (explosives) placard loads and other placard-quantity dangerous goods are:[^38]

1. **TDG training certificate** (s. 6.3 of SOR/2001-286) — issued by employer, 3-year validity
2. **ERAP confirmation** — the carrier or shipper must have an approved Emergency Response Assistance Plan for applicable quantities (Part 7 of SOR/2001-286)
3. **DG shipping documents** — per Part 3 of SOR/2001-286
4. **Emergency response information** in the cab (SOR/2001-286, Part 3)
5. **Placards and marks** on the vehicle (Part 4)

**Class-specific notes:**
- **Class 1 (Explosives):** Provincial explosives regulations (under the federal *Explosives Act*) may require additional authorization and inspection for vehicles carrying explosive substances, but this is a *carrier permit/authorization*, not a *driver training certificate*.
- **Class 2 (Gases) / Class 3 (Flammable Liquids) / Class 6 (Toxic):** No additional driver certificate required beyond Part 6 TDG training.
- The TDG certificate must specifically list the **aspects and DG classes** for which the employee is trained — a driver trained only for Class 3 is not qualified to handle Class 1 under their certificate.[^36]

**Requirement:** Federal — Transport Canada, TDG Directorate[^38]
**URL:** https://tc.canada.ca/en/dangerous-goods[^43]
**Confirmed current to:** March 17, 2026[^38]

> **Confidence Rating: 3 — Verified.** Part 6 and Part 7 of SOR/2001-286 reviewed directly.

## SECTION G — DRIVER ABSTRACT REQUIREMENTS (NSC Standard 15)

***

### G — Governing Standard

**NSC Standard 15** (*Requirement for the Review of Driving Abstracts*) requires commercial motor carriers to obtain and review driver abstracts:
- **At the time of hire:** A copy of the driver's abstract dated within **30 days** of the date the driver's employment begins[^44][^45]
- **Annually:** Updated copies of the driver abstract must be reviewed annually for all employed commercial drivers[^46][^45]

NSC Standard 15 is adopted into carrier safety fitness requirements in every province and territory; compliance is verified through carrier profile audits.[^40]

## SECTION H — OVERSIZED / OVERWEIGHT PILOT VEHICLE OPERATORS

***

### H — Alberta (AB)

**Training legislated?** **No** — as of the most recent verified source (2019 WCS Permits reference, confirmed by APTA operational practice), **Alberta does not have a mandatory government-legislated pilot escort vehicle certification** requirement. Pilot escort vehicle operation is not regulated by a provincial exam or licence.[^53]

However, **industry training programs** exist and are widely used:
- **Alberta Pilot Truck Alliance (APTA)** — provides competency-based training and certification for Escort Vehicle Operators. APTA pilots complete in-depth classroom training using the Canadian Pilot Truck Training Program and **over 140 hours of on-road training**.[^54]
- **Canadian Pilot Truck Training (CPTT)** — the PEVO (Pilot Escort Vehicle Operator) certification program; 32 hours classroom + 128 haul hours practical.[^55]
- Alberta Transportation's *Escort Vehicle Operator's Handbook* is the reference standard: https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/e7e17bc6-80e2-425a-a915-56c30716d281[^56]

> **Note:** Permit conditions issued by Alberta Transportation for specific oversize/overweight moves may specify training requirements as a permit condition, but this is per-permit, not a provincial exam-based licence.

***

### H — British Columbia (BC)

**Training legislated?** **No mandatory certification** for pilot car operators in BC. However, as of **February 15, 2017**, BC permit conditions require that:
1. **Pilot car operation** must comply with BC's *Pilot Car Load Movement Guidelines (PCLMG)*
2. **Traffic control functions** (slowing/stopping traffic) require a **certified Traffic Control Person (TCP)** certificate. Out-of-province pilot car operators performing traffic control must be certified in BC or provide proof of government-approved flagging training from their home jurisdiction.[^57][^58]

**BC PCLMG reference:** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/driving-and-transportation/transportation-infrastructure/engineering-standards-and-guidelines[^59]

***

### H — Ontario (ON)

**Training legislated?** **No mandatory certification** for escort vehicle operators. Ontario's oversize permit conditions specify minimum requirements for escort operators, including:
- Minimum age: **18 years**
- Driving experience: **at least 2 years**
- Valid driver's licence equivalent to at least an Ontario Class **G2** or higher
- The escort vehicle must be operated in accordance with the Ministry of Transportation permit conditions[^60][^61]

No formal pilot car training program or government exam is required in Ontario; the requirements are set as permit conditions, not a separate provincial certification.[^60]

## SECTION I — COR PROGRAMS FOR TRUCKING — NATIONAL CORA LIST

***

### I — COR Program Overview

The Certificate of Recognition (COR) is nationally trademarked and endorsed by the **Canadian Federation of Construction Safety Associations (CFCSA)**. It is delivered by industry-specific **Certifying Partners** (CORAs) under formal MOUs with their respective provincial WCB/workers' compensation board. COR is **voluntary** in all provinces for trucking employers (it is not legislated as a mandatory requirement for trucking carriers, though some industry clients require it for vendor qualification).[^62][^63]

***

### I1 — Alberta

**CORA for trucking:** **AMTA (Alberta Motor Transport Association)**  
**COR Program Status:** Confirmed **active**.[^64][^65]

- COR available to companies with **11 or more employees**; smaller employers should inquire about Small Employer COR (SECOR) programs through WCB.[^65]
- To achieve COR: Register with WCB Partnerships in Injury Reduction (PIR) program → Complete AMTA's 2-day *Health and Safety System Building (HSSB)* course → Develop and implement HSMS → Arrange external audit by a Certified AMTA Consultant Auditor (minimum 80% overall; no element below 50%) → Submit to AMTA for review.[^65]
- Full 3-year COR certification now available through re-certification audits (reinstated 2024).[^64]

**URL:** https://amta.ca/what-we-do/cor/cor-program/[^65]
**COR resources:** https://www.amta.ca/cor/resources[^66]

> **Note:** In 2025, AMTA's legacy *Partners in Compliance* carrier recognition program was replaced by the **Alberta government's Alberta Carrier Excellent Safety (ACES)** program. The ACES program is a separate carrier compliance recognition initiative from Alberta Transportation, distinct from the WCB-based COR program.[^67]

***

### I2 — British Columbia

**CORA for trucking:** **SafetyDriven — Trucking Safety Council of BC (TSCBC)**  
**COR Program Status:** Confirmed **active**.[^68][^62]

SafetyDriven is a certified partner with WorkSafeBC for the transportation, warehousing, and related sectors. The COR program is available to all employers in the General Trucking / Moving and Storage WCB classification and naturally aligned industries.[^62][^68]

**URL:** https://safetydriven.ca/certificate-of-recognition-cor/[^69]
**COR certified companies list:** https://safetydriven.ca/cor-certified-companies/[^68]
**2025 Workplan:** Confirmed published January 2025.[^70]

***

### I3 — Saskatchewan

**Trucking-specific CORA:** The **Saskatchewan Trucking Association (STA)** offers COR training and supports carriers in COR certification, but the STA is not a full-certification CORA in the same sense as AMTA or SafetyDriven.[^71][^72]

Saskatchewan trucking employers seeking COR may pursue it through:
- **Saskatchewan Construction Safety Association (SCSA)** — the primary CFCSA-affiliated COR provider in SK, but serves construction industry[^73]
- **Energy Safety Canada (ESC)** — for oilfield/energy sector employers[^73]

For general trucking employers in SK, the **WCB Saskatchewan Safety Certificate of Recognition** equivalent is the **Saskatchewan Safety Management System (SMS)** program, but no single trucking-specific CORA equivalent to AMTA or SafetyDriven has been confirmed as of May 2026.

**STA programs URL:** https://sasktrucking.com/programs/[^72]

> **Confidence Rating: 2 — Probable.** STA COR training services confirmed; however, confirmation of SK trucking employers' specific CORA designation requires direct verification with WCB Saskatchewan.

***

### I4 — Manitoba

**Trucking-specific CORA:** **WorkSafely™ (Manitoba Heavy Construction Association — MHCA)** is the Authority Having Jurisdiction for COR in Manitoba, but it primarily serves heavy construction (WCB codes 407/408).[^63][^74]

For **trucking employers** in Manitoba, the applicable CORA is the **Manitoba Construction Safety Association (CSAM)** or contact **Safe Work Manitoba / WCB Manitoba** directly to determine which certifying partner serves general trucking (WCB industry codes for trucking).[^63]

**CSAM URL:** https://www.constructionsafety.ca/[^74]
**WorkSafely / MHCA COR URL:** https://mhca.mb.ca/worksafely/cor/about-the-cor-program/[^63]

> **Confidence Rating: 2 — Probable.** No Manitoba trucking-specific CORA confirmed. Carriers should confirm directly with WCB Manitoba which certifying partner covers their WCB classification.

***

### I5 — Ontario

**CORA for trucking:** **IHSA (Infrastructure Health and Safety Association)** is confirmed as the **exclusive provider of COR in Ontario**.[^75][^76]

IHSA is the sole COR certifying body in Ontario under the CFCSA framework. It covers construction and naturally aligned industries. **Trucking employers in Ontario** may apply to IHSA for COR, particularly those whose operations involve construction site deliveries or material supply. However, IHSA's primary sector is construction.

**Current status note:** As of October 25, 2024, the Ontario Ministry of Labour directed IHSA to resume issuing COR certification for IAF-accredited ISO 45001 organizations (following a brief suspension of this pathway).[^77]

**IHSA COR URL:** https://www.ihsa.ca/cor-home[^76]

> **Practical note for trucking carriers:** Many Ontario trucking employers outside the construction sector pursue COR through IHSA under the "Naturally Aligned" or heavy truck category. Confirm eligibility directly with IHSA at ihsa.ca/cor.

***

### I6 — Quebec, NB, NS, NL, NT/NU, YT

**Quebec:** No trucking-specific CORA equivalent in Quebec. The CTQ/SAAQ carrier registration and mandatory maintenance record system serves as the provincial compliance framework. The IRSST (Institut de recherche Robert-Sauvé en santé et sécurité du travail) supports OHS programs but does not issue COR.[^78]

**New Brunswick:** No dedicated trucking CORA confirmed. Employers may use the **New Brunswick Federation of Labour** safety programs or contact WorkSafeNB.[^27]

**Nova Scotia:** No trucking-specific CORA confirmed. WorkSafe NS administers OHS. Contact: https://www.worksafens.ca[^29]

**Newfoundland & Labrador:** No trucking-specific CORA confirmed. WorkplaceNL administers workers' compensation. Contact: https://www.workplacenl.ca[^32]

**NT/NU/YT:** No trucking-specific COR programs confirmed for northern territories. WCB Northwest Territories/Nunavut and Yukon Workers' Compensation Health and Safety Board administer OHS programs; COR infrastructure is not as developed as in western provinces.

## KEY GAPS, CAVEATS AND COMPLIANCE NOTES

1. **GCWR thresholds — Class 1:** Alberta's Class 1 GCWR trigger is defined by trailer equipment (air brakes present + tractor-trailer configuration) rather than a specific kg GCWR number in the same format as SK (4,600 kg towed), ON (4,600 kg towed), and MB (4,540 kg towed). The 4,600 kg towed-unit threshold is the most common across SK, BC (Class 3 threshold for non-Class 1), MB, ON, and NB. Auditors should confirm Alberta's specific application with the Alberta Vehicle Inspection / Driver Fitness offices.

2. **Nova Scotia Class 3 threshold (14,000 kg):** NS uses a 14,000 kg GVW threshold for its Class 3 — this is significantly different from other western provinces that use 4,500–4,600 kg towed-unit weight thresholds. Multi-province fleets must ensure NS Class 3 licence holders meet the correct NS standard.

3. **Air brake endorsement — Ontario renewal (September 1, 2023 change):** The online Air Brake Learning Module (MTO SkillsPass) is now required before renewing the Z endorsement in Ontario. Carriers with Ontario-licensed commercial drivers must track renewal requirements.[^20]

4. **Alberta Class 1 Learning Pathway (October 1, 2025):** MELT is retired. New drivers must enter the Class 1 Learning Pathway. Existing MELT-trained drivers are grandfathered. Carriers recruiting new AB Class 1 drivers should familiarize themselves with the new apprenticeship-based pathway documentation requirements.[^8][^7]

5. **COR in SK, MB, QC:** No trucking-specific CORA equivalent to AMTA (AB) or SafetyDriven (BC) has been confirmed for SK, MB, or QC. Carriers in those provinces pursuing COR should contact their provincial WCB directly to identify the appropriate certifying partner for their WCB industry classification code.

6. **IHSA COR — Ontario Trucking:** IHSA is the sole Ontario COR provider. Trucking employers should verify with IHSA that their WCB NAICS/classification falls within IHSA's scope before beginning the COR process.[^76]
