#### A1(c) — NSC Standard 10: Cargo Securement

**Answer:** NSC Standard 10 (*Cargo Securement*) was last amended in **June 2013**. The June 2013 version, incorporating the 2010 amendment, is the current operative version and forms part of the March 2022 NSC Full Set.[^7][^2]

**Post-2022 enforcement note:** An amendment to NSC Standard 10, **Section 12(9)**, regarding the Working Load Limit (WLL) of rubber friction mats has been published and is now being actively enforced. The amendment eliminates the default WLL for unmarked friction mats (previously assumed at 50% of cargo weight) and now requires friction mats to be marked by the manufacturer with the maximum usable friction resistance (in g's). This change was endorsed following the CVSA Cargo Securement Public Forum and FMCSA review.[^7]

- **Previous text (Section 12(9)):** "A friction mat which is not marked by the manufacturer with a working load limit is assumed to provide resistance to horizontal movement equal to 50% of the weight of the cargo resting on the mat."
- **Current text (Section 12(9)):** "To be considered part of a cargo securement system, a friction mat must be marked by its manufacturer with the maximum usable friction resistance (in g's) the mat will provide in restraining cargo against horizontal and lateral movement."[^7]
- **Revision history on CCMTA page:** Amended June 2013; Amended September 2010; Introduced September 2004[^2]
- **Source:** CCMTA NSC Standard 10 page: https://www.ccmta.ca/en/national-safety-code  
- **BC CVSE archived version:** https://www.cvse.ca/nacs/NSC_10_Cargo%20Securement.pdf (June 2013 version)[^8]

> **Confidence Rating: 3 — Verified.** Both the CCMTA revision history and third-party enforcement notices confirm the June 2013 version is current, with the friction mat enforcement change now active.[^7][^2]

#### A2(d) — Short-Haul Exemption Under Section 77(3)

**Answer:** The short-haul exemption under **s. 77(3) of SOR/2005-313** is confirmed as **current law** and has not been amended since the June 12, 2021 consolidation.[^10][^9]

**Requirements for s. 77(3) exemption (current):**
- Driver operates within a **160 km radius** of the home terminal
- Driver **returns to the home terminal** at the end of each day
- Driver receives **at least 8 consecutive hours** off-duty before the next on-duty period
- If a driver goes outside the 160 km radius at any time during a day, an ELD and full daily log are required for that day[^18][^10]

No proposed amendments to the short-haul exemption are publicly listed on the Canada Gazette or Transport Canada websites as of May 2026.[^9]

- **Source:** SOR/2005-313, s. 77(3), Justice Canada, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2005-313/FullText.html  
- **Plain-language reference:** Transport Canada ELD Handout for Motor Carriers and Drivers, https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2022-10/electornic-logging-devices-commercial-vehicles-factsheet_r14.pdf[^10]

> **Confidence Rating: 3 — Verified.** The s. 77(3) short-haul threshold is confirmed in the Transport Canada factsheet and consistent across multiple sources.

### A3. TRANSPORTATION OF DANGEROUS GOODS — SITE REGISTRATION

#### A3(a) — SOR/2023-206 Phase 2 Site Registration: Current Requirements and Portal

**Answer:** **SOR/2023-206** (*Regulations Amending the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations (Site Registration Requirements)*) was registered on **October 6, 2023** and published in *Canada Gazette, Part II, Volume 157, Number 22, on October 25, 2023*.[^27][^28]

**Requirements:**
- The Regulations add **Part 17 (Site Registration Requirements)** to the *Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations* (SOR/2001-286).
- Under **section 17.3**, any person who imports, offers for transport, handles, or transports dangerous goods at a site in Canada that they own or operate **must register in the Client Identification Database (CID)** on the Transport Canada website.
- A **12-month transitional period** was built in: persons conducting DG activities as of the October 25, 2023 coming-into-force date had until **October 25, 2024** to complete initial registration.[^29][^30]
- Registration must be **renewed annually** within 30 days of the anniversary date of initial registration (s. 17.4).
- Information changes must be updated within **60 days** of any change (s. 17.5).[^28]

**Mandatory registration deadline:** October 25, 2024 (now passed — mandatory ongoing).[^31][^29]

**Active Portal URLs:**
- **TDG CID registration portal:** https://tc.canada.ca/en/dangerous-goods/client-identification-database-cid[^32]
- **Initial registration page:** https://tc.canada.ca/en/dangerous-goods/client-identification-database-cid/initial-registration[^32]
- **TDG Online Portal (umbrella):** https://tc.canada.ca/en/dangerous-goods/tdg-online[^33]
- **Canada Gazette source instrument:** https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2023/2023-10-25/html/sor-dors206-eng.html[^28]

**Exemptions from Part 17 registration include:** persons handling only excepted quantities; persons covered by specific special cases in Part 1; cross-border carriers without Canadian headquarters or sites; federal/provincial/municipal officers; persons operating oil wells (note: this exempts *extraction* wells but NOT petroleum terminals, refineries, or distribution centres).[^28]

> **Confidence Rating: 3 — Verified.** SOR/2023-206 full text reviewed. CID portal URLs confirmed from TC website and industry association sources.[^29][^33][^32]

#### A4(b) — CVSA Level 1 Inspection as Canadian Standard

**Answer:** **CVSA Level I (North American Standard Inspection)** remains the standard comprehensive roadside inspection applied in Canada. It includes examination of the driver's licence, medical examiner's certificate, hours of service records (ELD/logs), drugs and alcohol, vehicle components (brakes, lights, tires, steering, etc.), and dangerous goods documentation.[^43]

There have been **no post-2022 structural changes** to the Canadian CVSA inspection protocol or levels. The 2025 OOSC updates modify specific defect criteria thresholds within the established Level I–VI framework, not the framework itself.[^39][^38]

- **CVSA inspection levels reference:** https://cvsa.org/inspections/all-inspection-levels/[^43]
- **Confirmed:** CVSA and Transport Canada websites, May 2026[^14][^43]

### B — Specific Provincial Questions

#### B1. Ontario — CVOR

**Answer:** **Ontario's CVOR (Commercial Vehicle Operator's Registration)** is confirmed as **distinct from** a Safety Fitness Certificate (SFC). Ontario operates its own safety fitness system under the *Highway Traffic Act* (HTA), not under the federal *Motor Carrier Safety Fitness Certificate Regulations* (SOR/2005-180). The CVOR identifies the carrier and links all commercial vehicle collisions, convictions, and inspections to a single carrier record.[^48]

**CVOR thresholds:** Required for commercial motor vehicles (trucks, tractors, trailers, or combinations) with a registered gross weight or manufacturer's GVWR of more than 4,500 kg, and for buses with a seating capacity of more than 10.[^48]

**Current CVOR URLs:**
- **Carrier record online access (carriers only — free):** https://www.ontario.ca/carrierrecords (email on CVOR file required)[^56][^57]
- **Public abstract / search / order page:** https://www.ontario.ca/page/get-commercial-vehicle-operators-registration-cvor-abstract-or-carrier-record[^48]
- The public search tool provides: carrier search by name, list of unsatisfactory/cancelled carriers, Level 1 public CVOR abstract (2-year period).[^48]

**Source:** Ontario MTO / ServiceOntario, confirmed May 2026[^56][^48]

#### B2. Quebec — SAAQ and CTQ System

**Answer:** Quebec uses a **two-body system** for commercial carrier compliance:

1. **SAAQ (Société de l'assurance automobile du Québec)** administers vehicle registration, driver licensing, and mandatory periodic mechanical inspections of heavy vehicles.[^58][^59]

2. **CTQ (Commission des transports du Québec)** administers the **Register of Owners and Operators of Heavy Vehicles** (*Registre des propriétaires et des exploitants de véhicules lourds*). Mandatory registration in the CTQ register is required before operating a heavy vehicle (GVWR ≥ 4,500 kg) on Quebec roads, in lieu of an SFC.[^60][^49]

The CTQ issues an **attestation of registration** (not called a Safety Fitness Certificate), which serves as the carrier's operating authority in Quebec. Out-of-province Canadian carriers present their home-jurisdiction SFC or equivalent; only Quebec-registered operators and U.S. carriers must register directly with CTQ.[^49]

**URLs:**
- **CTQ main page (owners/operators):** https://www.ctq.gouv.qc.ca/english/owners-and-operators-of-heavy-vehicles/[^49]
- **CTQ obligations page:** https://www.ctq.gouv.qc.ca/english/owners-and-operators-of-heavy-vehicles/obligations/[^61]
- **SAAQ transportation of goods page:** https://saaq.gouv.qc.ca/en/transportation-goods[^62]
- **SAAQ registration of trucks over 3,000 kg:** https://saaq.gouv.qc.ca/en/vehicle-registration/registering-vehicle/truck-weighing-over-3000-kg[^59]

**Source:** CTQ and SAAQ websites, May 2026[^60][^49]

### C — Alberta CVIP Specific Questions

#### C(a) — Alberta CVIP Weight Threshold: 11,794 kg

**Answer:** **Confirmed correct.** The Alberta CVIP weight threshold of **11,794 kg registered GVW** is confirmed as the current threshold for **intra-provincial commercial vehicle operations**.[^64]

Specifically, from the **Alberta Transportation Vehicle Inspection Program — Commercial vehicles page** (https://www.alberta.ca/vehicle-inspection-program-commercial-vehicles):

> "All commercial vehicles, as defined by the National Safety Code (NSC), require annual commercial vehicle inspections: trucks, truck-tractors, semi-trailers, trailers and combinations thereof **exceeding a registered gross vehicle weight (GVW) of 4,500 kg when operating extra-provincially** [and] **exceeding a registered GVW of 11,794 kg when operating intra-provincially**."[^64]

The 11,794 kg threshold aligns with the International Registration Plan (IRP) threshold and was implemented May 1, 2004, lowering the previous threshold from 18,000 kg.[^73]

**Source:** Alberta Transportation, *Vehicle Inspection Program — Commercial Vehicles*, https://www.alberta.ca/vehicle-inspection-program-commercial-vehicles, confirmed May 2026.[^64]

> **Confidence Rating: 3 — Verified.** Alberta.ca official government page confirmed.

***

#### C(b) — Semi-Annual CVIP in Alberta: Which Vehicle Categories?

**Answer:** Semi-annual CVIP applies **only to buses** in Alberta. Commercial trucks, truck-tractors, semi-trailers, trailers and combinations are subject to **annual** CVIP only.[^64]

From the official Alberta Transportation page:

> "All buses, as defined by the NSC, require **semi-annual** commercial vehicle inspections: buses designed, constructed and used for the transportation of passengers with a designated seating capacity of **more than 10 (including the driver)**, but excluding the operation for personal use."[^64]

No other commercial vehicle category (tankers, flatdecks, dry vans, oilfield service vehicles, oversized/overweight configurations) is subject to semi-annual CVIP under Alberta Transportation's program.

**Source:** Alberta Transportation, *Vehicle Inspection Program — Commercial Vehicles*, https://www.alberta.ca/vehicle-inspection-program-commercial-vehicles[^64]

> **Confidence Rating: 3 — Verified.** Directly sourced from Alberta.ca government page.

## SECTION D — PROVINCIAL LAYER 2: OHS REGULATIONS FOR TRUCKING

***

### D — OHS Framework by Province

**General rule:** No Canadian province has a dedicated "trucking-only" OHS Act. The general provincial OHS legislation applies to the trucking sector. The question for each province is whether the OHS regulation contains any **transport-specific or mobile-equipment-specific Part** that trucking carriers must comply with in addition to general OHS requirements.

***

#### D — Alberta

**Regulatory instrument:** *Occupational Health and Safety Act* (RSA 2000, c. O-2.1), *OHS Regulation* (Alta. Reg. 62/2003), and *OHS Code* (Alta. Reg. 191/2021, as amended December 2024).[^74][^75]

**Trucking-relevant Parts of the OHS Code:**
- **Part 14** — *Lifting and Handling Loads*: applies to loading/unloading, manual material handling at carrier terminals[^76]
- **Part 16** — *Noise*: lowered threshold from 85 dBa to 82 dBa as of **March 31, 2023** (Alberta OHS Code amendment). Carriers with shop/terminal operations must formally assess for noise exposure under the new 82 dBa threshold.[^77]
- **Part 33** — *Explosives*: relevant for DG carriers[^78]
- **Part 37** — *Oil, gas and geothermal energy*: applies to oilfield service vehicles[^78]
- **No trucking-specific OHS Part** covers highway driving activities directly; the OHS Act does not apply to federally regulated carriers (those under the *Canada Labour Code*).[^75]

**December 2024 OHS Code amendments** (violence/harassment, explosives, oil & gas, miscellaneous) took effect December 2024.[^78]

**Source:** https://search-ohs-laws.alberta.ca/legislation/occupational-health-and-safety-code/[^74]

***

#### D — British Columbia

**Regulatory instrument:** *Workers Compensation Act* (RSBC 2019, c. 1) and *Occupational Health and Safety Regulation* (BC Reg. 296/97), administered by WorkSafeBC.

**Transport-specific Parts of BC OHS Regulation:**
- **Part 16 — Mobile Equipment**: Entirely repealed and re-enacted effective **September 1, 2021** (BC Reg. 207/2021 and 222/2021). Governs the safety of mobile equipment including commercial trucks, tractors, and powered industrial vehicles in workplaces. Covers pre-operational inspection, seating, ROPS/FOPS requirements, load handling, and operator training.[^79][^80]
  - **URL:** https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-16-mobile-equipment[^81]
  
- **Part 17 — Transportation of Workers**: Applies to all employers transporting **3 or more workers** by any type of conveyance operated on behalf of the employer. Sections 17.1–17.22 cover vehicle condition, driver qualifications, road/weather assessment, boarding/deboarding, fire extinguishers, and communication.[^82][^83]
  - **URL:** https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-17-transportation-of-workers[^83]
  - **Last updated:** Amended December 1, 2021 (section 17.10 vehicle design revised)[^79]
  - **Scope of Part 17:** Applies to transportation of workers to/from a workplace; it is **not** the primary vehicle safety framework for commercial goods carriers operating on highways (which is governed by the *Motor Vehicle Act* and CVSE regulations), but it does apply to oilfield service vehicles, camp transport, and carrier terminal shuttle operations that transport workers.[^82]

- **Part 18 — Traffic Control**: Applies to workers directing traffic around work zones including loading/unloading areas.[^84]

**Additional trucking OHS resources:** WorkSafeBC Commercial Trucking page: https://www.worksafebc.com/en/health-safety/industries/transportation/types/commercial-trucking[^85]

**Source:** WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation, confirmed May 2026[^83][^79]

***

#### D — Ontario

**Regulatory instrument:** *Occupational Health and Safety Act* (RSO 1990, c. O.1) and subordinate regulations.

**Trucking-relevant OHS Regulation:**
- **O. Reg. 851 — Industrial Establishments** (RRO 1990, Reg. 851): Applies to all industrial establishments, which include warehouses, freight terminals, loading docks, and trucking company facilities. Current consolidation period: July 1, 2022 to present; last amendment: O. Reg. 374/22.[^86][^87]
  - **URL:** https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/900851[^86]
  - **Key trucking/loading dock provisions:**
    - **Section 45** (*Material Handling*): Materials must be transported, placed, or stored so they will not tip, collapse, or fall and can be removed without endangering workers. Removal from a storage area must not endanger a worker. This provision directly governs loading dock operations, staging areas, and trailer loading at carrier terminals.[^88]
    - **Sections 45–67.71** (*Material Handling* part): the full block of material handling requirements applies to carrier terminal operations.[^86]
  - **No section of O. Reg. 851 is trucking-specific in the sense of governing on-highway operations.** The regulation covers the fixed workplace (terminal, warehouse, loading dock) but not the truck cab itself while in transit.
  
- **O. Reg. 555/06 — Hours of Service** (under the *Highway Traffic Act*, not OHSA): Governs HOS for Ontario's provincially regulated carriers. ELD mandatory for applicable drivers under O. Reg. 555/06 as of June 12, 2022.[^21]

- There is **no dedicated trucking-specific Part** within Ontario's OHSA framework equivalent to BC's Part 17.

**Source:** Ontario e-Laws, confirmed May 2026[^86]

***

#### D — Quebec

**Regulatory instrument:** *Act respecting occupational health and safety* (LSST — *Loi sur la santé et la sécurité du travail*, CQLR c. S-2.1), administered by CNESST (Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail). A major reform (Bill 59, passed September 30, 2021) has been phased in, with final permanent provisions of the reform entering force **October 1, 2025** under Order in Council 1154-2025.[^89][^90]

**Trucking-relevant provisions:**
- The LSST is a general OHS Act — no trucking-specific Part exists under the LSST.
- Heavy vehicle carrier compliance (including driver fatigue, vehicle condition, and trip inspection) is primarily governed under Quebec's *Act respecting transportation by taxi* framework for goods carriers and the SAAQ's mandatory periodic inspection and maintenance regime, not the LSST.[^68][^91]
- The CTQ register and SAAQ's maintenance record requirements (2-year retention for active vehicles) are the primary provincial compliance instruments for commercial carriers beyond federal requirements.[^91]

**Source:** CNESST, SAAQ, BLG analysis of Bill 59 reform[^90][^92]

***

#### D — Saskatchewan

**Regulatory instrument:** *Saskatchewan Employment Act* (SS 2013, c. S-15.1) and *Occupational Health and Safety Regulations, 2020* (Sask. Reg. S-15.1 Reg 10, effective April 1, 2021, as amended).[^93]

- No trucking-specific Part in Saskatchewan's OHS Regulations. General OHS applies to carrier terminals and workplaces.
- Driver safety on highways for intra-provincial carriers is primarily governed by the SGI's PMVI program, HOS regulations, and the NSC framework administered by SGI.[^66][^46]

**Source:** WorkSafe Saskatchewan, https://www.worksafesask.ca/industries/occupational-health-safety/; Saskatchewan OHS Regulations: https://pubsaskdev.blob.core.windows.net/pubsask-prod/126367/S15-1r10.pdf[^94][^93]

***

#### D — Manitoba

**Regulatory instrument:** *Workplace Safety and Health Act* (CCSM c. W210) and *Workplace Safety and Health Regulation* (Man. Reg. 217/2006), administered by Safe Work Manitoba / Workers Compensation Board of Manitoba. A significant amendment (Bill 29) entered force as of June 3, 2025.[^95][^96]

**Trucking-relevant provisions:**
- **Part 22 — Powered Mobile Equipment** (Man. Reg. 217/2006, Part 22): Applies to every workplace where powered mobile equipment (including commercial trucks and forklifts) is used. Covers safe work procedures, inspection and maintenance, operator's manual requirements, operator training and qualification, loading and unloading, transport of flammable materials in enclosed vehicles, and towing.[^97]
  - Section 22.13: Employer must ensure flammable liquids and hazardous chemicals are not transported in the enclosed part of a vehicle in a way that creates risk.[^97]
  - **URL:** https://www.wcb.mb.ca/topics/powered-mobile-equipment/[^98]
- The *Workplace Safety and Health Act* s. 45.3 (added May 30, 2023) requires workplace owners to provide washroom access to delivery persons attending the workplace for collection or delivery purposes.[^95]

**Source:** Manitoba Trucking Association, WCB Manitoba, Manitoba WSHA[^99][^95][^97]
