### Question 2 — Ontario: O. Reg. 312/03 (Logging)

#### (a) Is O. Reg. 312/03 the Primary Ontario Logging OHS Regulation?

**NO — CORRECTION REQUIRED.** O. Reg. 312/03 in Ontario is a regulation under the *Insurance Act* (Court proceedings for automobile accidents) — it is **not** an OHS logging regulation. This is a critical instrument misidentification.[^30]

**The correct primary Ontario logging OHS instruments are:**

1. **R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 851 — Industrial Establishments** (Sections 103–119 = Logging)
2. **O. Reg. 488/01** — amending Reg. 851, adding ss. 104–106.2 (cutter/skidder and mechanical harvesting operator training programs)[^31]

Both are made under the *Occupational Health and Safety Act*, R.S.O. 1990, c. O.1.

**Confidence:**  — O. Reg. 312/03 as non-logging instrument confirmed from official Ontario e-Laws.[^3]

#### (b) Current Consolidated Version and Amendments Since 2019

**R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 851** current consolidation: **From July 1, 2022 to the e-Laws currency date. Last amendment: O. Reg. 374/22.**[^27]

Legislative history of amendments: O. Reg. 516/92, 630/94, 230/95, 450/97, 144/99, 284/99, 528/00, 488/01, 280/05, 629/05, 565/06, 179/07, 494/09, 420/10, 98/11, 382/15, 289/17, 60/18, 456/18, **186/19**, 421/21, 434/21, **374/22**.[^27]

Post-2019 amendments to Reg. 851:
- **O. Reg. 186/19** (July 1, 2019): personal flotation device requirements, hazardous biological/chemical agent emergency treatment[^75]
- **O. Reg. 421/21** (July 1, 2021): revoked s. 51–53 notice and reporting requirements (consolidated into O. Reg. 420/21)[^76][^2]
- **O. Reg. 434/21**: pre-start health and safety review amendments[^27]
- **O. Reg. 374/22** (July 1, 2022): most recent amendment[^77]

No amendments to Reg. 851 specifically affecting logging sections 103–119 were identified post-2019 other than the reporting consolidation (O. Reg. 421/21).

**Confidence:**  for consolidation details;  for absence of logging-specific amendments post-2019 in ss. 103–119.[^2][^3]

#### (c) Chainsaw/Falling Safety Requirements

**YES.** Section **39** of R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 851 (within the general regulations, applicable to all industrial establishments including logging) contains chainsaw safety requirements:[^27]

> *A chain saw shall: (a) have (i) a chain that minimizes the possibility of a kickback, and (ii) a device which will effectively stop the chain in the event of a kickback; (b) be in safe operating condition; (c) when being started, be held firmly; (d) when being used, be held firmly by both hands; and (e) have the chain stopped when not actually cutting.*

Logging-specific provisions (ss. 103–119) cover: bucking operations, cutter training programs (ss. 104–106.2 via O. Reg. 488/01), log hauling, tree felling procedures, and equipment requirements.[^29][^31]

**Confidence:**[^3]

This regulation specifically governs silviculture and harvesting operations in Quebec, separate from the general *Regulation respecting occupational health and safety* (S-2.1, r.13).[^36]

**Confidence:**[^3]

#### (b) CNESST Regulation for Sylviculture and Harvesting

### Question 5 — BC: Remote Work / First Aid (Part 3)

#### (a) First Aid Level for Remote Logging Camps — Travel Time Thresholds

The BC OHS Regulation Part 3 was significantly amended effective **November 1, 2024** (B.C. Reg. 132/2023). The **previous** system based on explicit "2-hour" and "4-hour" travel time thresholds has been **replaced** by a new assessment-based system.[^81][^8][^7]

**Current framework (post-November 1, 2024):**

The new Part 3 establishes a **performance-based, two-part framework** under s. 3.16:[^82][^83][^81]

1. Employers must provide at minimum the equipment, supplies, facilities, first aid attendants, and services required by **Schedule 3-A** (minimum levels of first aid).
2. Employers must also provide any **additional** equipment/attendants/services determined necessary by a written **first aid assessment** conducted in consultation with workers.

**Schedule 3-A classification** for remote logging camps:

A "remote" workplace is defined as one where a **BC Emergency Health Services (BCEHS) ambulance would normally take more than 30 minutes to reach the workplace**.[^83][^84][^81]

The classification tables under Schedule 3-A are organized by:
- **Number of workers** present
- **Hazard rating** (low/moderate/high — logging = **high hazard**)
- **Workplace class** (Class 4 = remote/isolated)[^84]

For a **Class 4 (remote), high hazard workplace** such as a logging camp (10–19 workers):[^84]

> **Advanced First Aid Kit, Attendant, Emergency Transport** required.

For 6–9 workers at Class 4 high hazard: **Intermediate First Aid Kit, Attendant, Emergency Transport** required.[^84]

**Important transition note:** The former terminology of OFA Level 2 and OFA Level 3 has been **renamed** as of November 1, 2024:[^85][^86][^81]
- OFA Level 1 → **Basic First Aid** (1-day course)
- OFA Level 2 → **Intermediate First Aid** (2-day course, formerly 35-hour/5-day)
- OFA Level 3 → **Advanced First Aid** (10-day/70-hour course)

Existing Level 2 and Level 3 certificates remain valid until their natural expiry, up to November 1, 2027.[^81]

**On the specific "2-hour" and "4-hour" thresholds:** These thresholds appeared in the **prior version** of Part 3 and associated guidelines. The **current** regulation (post-Nov. 1, 2024) uses the "30-minute BCEHS ambulance" threshold to define remote, and the written assessment process determines additional requirements. The relevant **WorkSafeBC OHS Guidelines for Part 3 (June 2024 preliminary revision)** contain updated guidance on remote workplace first aid assessment. The prior guideline reference (OHS Guidelines, Part 3, G3.16) addressed remote workplaces with travel time >2 hours as requiring a first aid room with overnight care capability. Auditors should obtain the current Schedule 3-A directly and conduct a written assessment.[^87][^88][^83][^81]

**Confidence:**  for post-November 2024 framework;  for new terminology;  for 2-hour/4-hour thresholds being superseded (prior guideline reference confirmed but current version may differ).[^2][^3]

#### (b) OFA Level 2 or Level 3 Requirement for Remote Camps

Under the current (post-November 1, 2024) framework:
- A **remote (Class 4), high hazard** logging operation with 10+ workers requires an **Advanced First Aid attendant** (formerly OFA Level 3).[^84]
- Remote Class 4, high hazard, with 6–9 workers: **Intermediate First Aid attendant** (formerly OFA Level 2) plus Emergency Transport.[^84]
- For very small remote logging camps (2–5 workers, high hazard): **Intermediate First Aid** attendant plus Emergency Transport.[^84]

The employer's **written first aid assessment** may require a higher level than the Schedule 3-A minimum based on specific site conditions, travel time, and injury risk.

**Confidence:**  — Schedule 3-A table values cross-referenced from a secondary compliance source; auditors must verify directly against current Schedule 3-A at WorkSafeBC.[^2]

### Question 6 — Federal Environmental Overlays for Forestry

#### (a) Migratory Birds Convention Act (MBCA): Timing Windows and Enforcement

**Governing Statute:** *Migratory Birds Convention Act, 1994*, S.C. 1994, c. 22 (MBCA). Federal legislation — applies nationally across AB, BC, ON and all jurisdictions.[^90]

**General Nesting Period (national):** The federal general nesting period guidance covers **approximately March 1 to August 31** (Zone A3 nesting period markers at approximately April 13 and August 23 for peak zones).[^91][^92][^93][^94]

Specific guidance from Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC):
- **Canada.ca nesting periods map:** https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/avoiding-harm-migratory-birds/general-nesting-periods/nesting-periods.html[^93]
- Avoid tree and brush clearing during the General Nesting Period of **March 1 to August 31** to protect active nesting season of most birds.[^91]
- Activities that may constitute "incidental take" (contravention of MBCA) include: forest harvesting, stand thinning, brush removal, site clearing, and road construction during the nesting period.[^95]
- Year-round protected nests include eagles, peregrine falcons, gyrfalcons, osprey, great blue herons — nest surveys required year-round for these species before clearing.[^91]

**Enforcement:** **Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC)** enforces the MBCA federally. Provincial bodies do not enforce the MBCA but may enforce parallel provincial wildlife legislation. The MBCA's incidental take prohibition applies even when the harm is not intentional — "incidental take is a contravention of the MBCA."[^95]

**No specific province-specific timing windows** (distinct from the national framework) were identified in research for AB, BC, or ON under the MBCA itself. Provincial wildlife legislation (e.g., Alberta Wildlife Act, BC Wildlife Act, Ontario Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act) may impose additional or province-specific nesting restrictions.

**Confidence:**  for March 1–August 31 general nesting period and ECCC enforcement;  for absence of province-specific MBCA timing windows (provincial legislation may add specificity not captured here).[^2][^3]
