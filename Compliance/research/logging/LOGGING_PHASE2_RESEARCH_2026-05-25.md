### A.1 — BRITISH COLUMBIA: BC Faller Training Standard (BCFTS)

**Legislative Basis:** The BC faller certification requirement is **legislated** under the *Occupational Health and Safety Regulation*, B.C. Reg. 296/97, **ss. 26.22 (New Faller Training Program), 26.22.1 (Falling Supervisors), and 26.21 (Qualifications)**.[^4][^5]

**Trigger:** Section 26.22 of the OHS Regulation applies to any worker who **fells a tree larger than 6 inches (15 cm) in diameter** in a forestry operation.[^5][^6]

**Administering Body:** WorkSafeBC approves and BCFSC administers the **BC Faller Training Standard (BCFTS)**. The **BC Forest Safety Council (BCFSC)** and the **Canadian Association of Geophysical Contractors (CAGC)** are the authorized bodies for faller training and certification.[^4][^5]

#### Program Structure — BC Faller Training Standard

There is **NOT a numbered Level 1 / Level 2 / Level 3 system** as might be described informally. The BCFTS is organized by **pathway and role**, not by numbered level:[^5][^4]

| Credential | Description | Who Needs It |
|---|---|---|
| **BC Faller Training Standard — Certified Faller** | Full production falling certification. Required for all hand fallers in forestry operations felling trees >6 inches diameter. | Production hand fallers (commercial harvesting operations) |
| **BC Certified Falling Supervisor (CFS)** | Supervisory credential for falling operations. Oversees trainees and certified fallers. | Falling supervisors |
| **BC Qualified Faller Trainer (QFT)** | Authorization to train faller trainees. | Trainers delivering New Faller Training Program |
| **Basic Chainsaw Operator Training** | Entry-level saw handling (bucking on level terrain only). Does NOT qualify for tree falling or steep terrain bucking. Arranged through authorized trainer Steve Telosky. | General forest workers needing basic chainsaw operation — NOT production fallers |

**Separate entry-level chainsaw course (BCFSC Basic Chainsaw Operator Training)** is an **industry course only** — not legislated under OHS Reg. It covers: saw maintenance, chain filing, safe cutting techniques, basic bucking on level terrain. Duration: 2 days, field-focused. Does not confer faller certification. Participants receive a wallet card.[^7]

#### Paths to Certified Faller Status

**Path 1 — New Faller Trainee (s. 26.22):**[^4][^5]
1. Complete the **30-day New Faller Training Program** (BCFSC-administered)
2. Complete up to **180 days of on-the-job training** under a Qualified Supervisor or Certified Faller
3. Submit minimum **20 Faller Trainee Weekly Training and Progress Reports** within 2 years preceding application (last 5 reports must indicate meeting the Standard)
4. Obtain written supervisor recommendation
5. Apply for and pass BCFSC certification assessment (additional fee)

**Path 2 — Out-of-Jurisdiction Faller (OHS Guideline G26.22(7)):**[^4]
- Must provide verifiable training records from a recognized training agency
- Must evidence 2 years (minimum 180 days = 90 days/year) of regular production falling within the last 2 years

#### Renewal Period

**BCFSC Faller Certification Cards** are issued every **3 years** to certified fallers in good standing with the BCFSC. (Cards were issued annually up to January 2020; changed to 3-year cycle.)[^4]

### A.2 — ONTARIO: Cutter/Skidder Training Certification

**Legislative Basis:** **LEGISLATED under O. Reg. 488/01**, which amended R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 851, ss. 104–106.2 (Logging sections of Industrial Establishments regulation). This is a **mandatory training certification requirement** for cutters and skidder operators.[^9][^10]

> ⚠️ *Reminder: O. Reg. 312/03 is NOT a logging regulation. The correct instruments are Reg. 851 ss. 103–119 and O. Reg. 488/01.*

**Trigger (s. 104(1), as amended):** Sections 105 to 106.2 apply to all employers who undertake logging operations.[^9]

**Issuing Body:** Programs are registered with the **Ministry of Training, Colleges and Universities** (now Ministry of Colleges and Universities / Ontario's Skilled Trades College framework). "Registered" means registered with the Ministry in order to complete the relevant training program.[^9]

#### Mandatory Training Programs (s. 105 of Reg. 851 as amended by O. Reg. 488/01)

**For Cutters and Skidder Operators:**[^9]

| Program | Program Number |
|---|---|

| Cutter (standalone) | P750010 |

**For Mechanical Harvesting Equipment Operators:**[^9]

| Program | Program Number |
|---|---|

**Registration Obligation (s. 106(2)):** Every cutter or skidder operator who has not completed the required training must be registered for the appropriate program **before** performing work to which the program relates. Employer must ensure successful completion within **1 year** of registration.[^9]

**Inter-provincial Recognition (s. 105(3)):** Workers who have completed equivalent training in another province or territory shall be deemed to hold equivalent certification, per the *Agreement on Internal Trade, 1995*.[^9]

**Is there a separate chainsaw certification like BC's?** No. Ontario does **not** have a separate chainsaw certification program analogous to BC's BCFTS. The cutter training programs (P750000, P750010) cover chainsaw/hand falling operations for logging workers. The general chainsaw safety requirement (Reg. 851, s. 39) applies to all industrial establishments but is not a named certification program.[^11][^9]

### A.3 — ALBERTA: Chainsaw/Falling Safety — Employer Competency Only

**Legislative Basis:** There is **no named Alberta chainsaw certification program** and no legislated third-party faller certification equivalent to BC's BCFTS.[^12][^13]

**Requirement Type:** **Performance-based employer competency requirement** under the *Occupational Health and Safety Code*, Alberta Regulation 191/2021.

**Relevant provisions of the OHS Code, Part 34 — Forestry:**[^13][^12]

- **s. 518** — General felling and bucking requirements
- **s. 519** — Hand felling: "A falling tree must never be allowed to strike a worker. Enough space needs to be provided so that workers can easily get out of the way when a tree falls." Chainsaw safe work procedures and distances from other workers.
- **s. 520** — Mechanized feller or limber

**General competency framework:** OHS Code, s. 10(2): *"A worker shall be competent to perform the work assigned to them."* The employer must ensure workers are competent. Competency must be demonstrable but does not require a named third-party certification.[^13]

**Does AFPA Issue a Chainsaw Certificate?** AFPA is a COR Certifying Partner and provides **Leadership in Health and Safety (LHS)** training for OHS management system purposes — it does not issue a named chainsaw/faller certification program. AFPA provides guidance resources and safety training but no formal provincial chainsaw credential exists.[^14][^15][^12]

**Official URL — Part 34:** https://search-ohs-laws.alberta.ca/legislation/occupational-health-and-safety-code/part-34-forestry/[^13]

**Confidence:**  for no named Alberta chainsaw certification;  for employer competency framework under Part 34;  for AFPA not issuing chainsaw certificates.[^3]

## SECTION B — CABLE YARDING AND TOWER LOGGING

### B.1 — BC: Cable Yarding Operator Qualification

**Legislative Basis:** OHS Regulation, Part 26 — Sections governing yarding operations (s. 26.40 et seq.).[^22][^23]

**Requirement Type:** **Employer competency requirement** — NOT a named third-party operator certification program. Part 26 does not require yarding operators to hold a specific named certification card analogous to the BC Faller Training Standard.[^23][^22]

**What Part 26 requires for yarding:**[^22][^23]
- s. 26.1 / s. 26.2: All workers in a forestry operation must receive the training necessary to safely perform their duties
- The requirements of s. 26.2(1) are met by completing training programs established or approved by the Board (WorkSafeBC)
- Employers must ensure grapple yarder/swing yarder operators are competent, familiar with manufacturer's operating instructions, and able to perform safe rigging and yarding procedures

**WorkSafeBC Grapple Yarder Operator Skills (Unit 1021):** The BCFSC publishes a competency unit document "Apply Grapple Yarder Operator Skills" (Unit 1021) which describes the required skills and knowledge standard for grapple yarder operators. This is a **competency standard, not a certification** — it defines what an operator must be able to demonstrate. Employer documents the competency assessment.[^23]

**No renewal period** is specified because this is not a certificate — employer must ensure ongoing competency and supervisor/qualified person oversight during yarding operations.[^22]

**Official URL — Unit 1021:** https://www.bcforestsafe.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/lng_1021_Apply_Grapple_Yarder_Operator_Skills-2.pdf[^23]

**Confidence:**  for employer competency requirement;  for no named third-party yarding certification;  for Unit 1021 as the defining skills standard (document confirmed but current regulatory status of this unit should be verified with BCFSC).[^3][^2]

## SECTION C — FIRST AID REQUIREMENTS: REMOTE LOGGING CAMPS (>2 hours from hospital)

### C.1 — British Columbia

**Current Framework (in force November 1, 2024 — B.C. Reg. 132/2023):** The prior system tied to explicit "2-hour" and "4-hour" travel-time thresholds has been **replaced** by a performance-based system under s. 3.16 of the OHS Regulation, keyed to Schedule 3-A.[^24][^25]

**New framework triggers:** A workplace is "remote" if a **BCEHS (BC Emergency Health Services) ambulance would normally take more than 30 minutes to reach the workplace**.[^25]

**Nomenclature change (November 1, 2024):**[^24]

| Old Name | New Name | Course Length |
|---|---|---|
| OFA Level 1 | Basic First Aid | Standard course |
| OFA Level 2 | Intermediate First Aid | 2-day course (replaced former 5-day/35-hour OFA 2) |
| OFA Level 3 | Advanced First Aid | 10-day/70-hour course |

Existing OFA Level 2 and Level 3 certificates remain valid until their natural expiry, up to **November 1, 2027**.[^24]

**Schedule 3-A — Remote, High Hazard Logging Camps:**[^26][^25]

| Workers on Shift | Workplace Class | Minimum Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 2–5 | Class 4 (remote), High Hazard | Intermediate First Aid (formerly OFA Level 2) + Emergency Transport |
| 6–9 | Class 4 (remote), High Hazard | Intermediate First Aid + Emergency Transport |
| 10–19 | Class 4 (remote), High Hazard | **Advanced First Aid** (formerly OFA Level 3) + Emergency Transport |
| 20+ | Class 4 (remote), High Hazard | Advanced First Aid + Emergency Transport + First Aid Room |

*Logging is classified as **High Hazard** under WorkSafeBC hazard classification.*[^25]

**Renewal Periods (post-November 1, 2024):**[^24]
- **Basic First Aid (OFA L1 equivalent):** Retake full course — 3-year validity
- **Intermediate First Aid (OFA L2 equivalent):** Retake full course — 3-year validity[^27]
- **Advanced First Aid (OFA L3 equivalent):** 35-hour refresher class, OR examination challenge (final 14 hours, must hold valid/unexpired certificate + CPR/AED within 6 months) — 3-year validity[^24]

**Approved OFA Training Providers:** WorkSafeBC maintains an official list of approved first aid training providers (public and in-house), updated regularly.[^28][^29]

Selected major public providers in BC (from WorkSafeBC-approved list, updated March 2026):[^28]
- ABCB First Aid Training Inc. (Nanaimo)
- Academy of Emergency Training (North Vancouver)
- Alert First-Aid (Victoria, Nanaimo, Vancouver, Duncan)
- Alpha Training Solutions (Fort St. John — important for northern logging camps)
- Arcticstar First Aid (Prince George — important for interior logging areas)

**Official URLs:**[^25][^28][^24]
- **First Aid Requirements (WorkSafeBC):** https://www.worksafebc.com/en/health-safety/create-manage/first-aid-requirements
- **First Aid Attendant Certification:** https://www.worksafebc.com/en/health-safety/education-training-certification/first-aid-attendant
- **Approved Training Providers List (live, March 2026):** https://www.worksafebc.com/en/health-safety/education-training-certification/first-aid-attendant/find-training
- **OHS Regulation Part 3 (searchable):** https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-03-rights-and-responsibilities

### C.2 — Alberta

**Governing Regulation:** OHS Code, Alberta Regulation 191/2021, **Part 11 — First Aid** (ss. 178–184 and Schedule 2), in force **March 31, 2023**.[^30][^12]

**Threshold for Remote/Isolated Workplace:** Part 11 uses a travel-time framework. For a workplace classified as **"isolated"** (generally defined as more than **2 hours** surface travel from a hospital), the employer must provide a first aid attendant with higher qualification. - **50+ workers, isolated, high hazard:** Emergency Medical Responder or higher

### C.3 — Saskatchewan

**Governing Regulation:** *OHS Regulations, 2020*, R.R.S. c. S-15.1 Reg 10, **Part 5 — First Aid**.[^31][^32]

**Threshold and Levels (from official First Aid in Saskatchewan Workplaces guide):**[^32]

Saskatchewan uses three distance classifications:
- **Close** = ≤30 minutes to medical facility
- **Distant** = 30 min – 2 hours to medical facility
- **Isolated** = **More than 2 hours' surface transport to medical facility, OR normal mode of transport is aircraft**

**Logging is listed as explicit HIGH HAZARD work** in Saskatchewan.[^32]

For a **remote/isolated logging camp** (>2 hours to medical facility, high hazard):

| Workers | Minimum Requirement |
|---|---|
| 2–4 workers | Class A attendant + blankets, stretcher, splints |
| 5–9 workers | Class A attendant + additional supplies + stretcher |
| 10–20 workers | Class A attendant + additional supplies + stretcher |
| 21–40 workers | Class B attendant (high hazard) + additional supplies + stretcher |
| 41–99 workers | EMT (high hazard) + additional supplies + stretcher |
| 100+ workers | First aid room + 1 EMT + 1 Class B attendant (high hazard) + stretcher |

**Level Definitions:**[^32]
- **Class A attendant:** Minimum 18 hours combined first aid and CPR training; 3-year renewal
- **Class B attendant:** Minimum 68 hours first aid and CPR training; 3-year renewal
- **EMT:** Licensed Emergency Medical Technician in Saskatchewan

## SECTION D — PESTICIDE APPLICATOR LICENSING FOR SILVICULTURE

### D.1 — Federal PMRA vs. Provincial Applicator Licences

**Federal Role:** The *Pest Control Products Act*, S.C. 2002, c. 28 (PCPA) governs **pesticide registration, labelling, and marketing** in Canada. All silviculture pesticides must be federally registered under the PCPA before provincial use.[^37][^38][^39]

**Provincial Role:** Provinces regulate the **application, use, transportation, storage, and disposal** of pesticides, including applicator licensing. A valid provincial pesticide applicator licence satisfies the provincial requirement but does **not** replace or substitute for federal PCPA registration of the pesticide product itself.[^40][^39]

**In short:** PCPA is product registration; provincial licence is applicator authorization. **Both are required.** The PMRA does not issue individual applicator licences — this is entirely a provincial function.[^38][^39]

## SECTION G — WILDLIFE AWARENESS TRAINING

### G.1 — Is There a Legislated Wildlife Awareness Requirement in BC, AB, or YT?

#### British Columbia

**Legislative Basis:** There is **NO named legislated wildlife awareness certification program** in BC for forestry workers. The requirement is **performance-based under the general hazard assessment framework** of the OHS Regulation, Part 2 (ss. 4.1–4.53 — Workplace Hazard Assessment and Control).[^61]

WorkSafeBC's bulletin "Plan for and prevent encounters with bears and other hazardous wildlife" describes the employer duty to:[^61]
- Identify and assess wildlife hazards through the Part 2 hazard assessment process
- Control risks (camp location, food storage, pairing workers, check-ins, communications)
- Provide wildlife deterrents (bear spray, bear bangers, air horns)
- Educate and train workers on bear identification, signs, deterrents, encounter response
- Train supervisors in wildlife risk assessment

**This is an employer obligation under s. 4.2 and s. 4.3 of Part 2 — not a named certification program.** No minimum training duration, formal certificate, or issuing body is specified by WorkSafeBC regulation.

**WorkSafeBC URL:** https://www.worksafebc.com/resources/health-safety/bulletins/plan-prevent-encounters-bears-other-hazardous-wildlife[^61]

**Confidence:**  for no legislated wildlife cert;  for employer duty under OHS Reg. Part 2.[^3]

***

#### Alberta

**Legislative Basis:** No named legislated wildlife awareness certification program in AB OHS Code.[^62]

The requirement is **employer-driven** under OHS Code, Part 2 (Hazard Assessment, Elimination and Control). Multiple Alberta training providers offer Wildlife Awareness programs (e.g., ACTI Wildlife Awareness Level 1 and Level 2) that align with OH&S regulations and industry practices, but these are **industry-standard programs, not legislated certifications**.[^62]

**Industry Standard:** Some major AB operators (e.g., Suncor) **require** Wildlife Aware Level 2 for all contractors on remote sites — this is a contractual/employer-mandated requirement, not a statutory one.[^63]

**ACTI Wildlife Awareness Level 2** covers: bear awareness, cougars, wolves, moose, foxes, legislation and OHS compliance, wildlife deterrents. This program "aligns with OHS regulations and industry best practices" but is not mandated by regulation.[^62]

**Confidence:**  for no legislated cert;  for employer/contractual model in AB.[^3]

***

#### Yukon

**Legislative Basis:** No named legislated wildlife awareness certification program under the *Workers' Safety and Compensation Act*, S.Y. 2021, c. 15 or the Workplace Health and Safety Regulations.[^64][^65]

As with BC and AB, the requirement is performance-based under general hazard identification and control provisions of YT OHS legislation. Wildlife risk in Yukon logging and remote work environments is managed through employer hazard programs and site-specific plans.

**Confidence:**  for no legislated cert;  for Yukon employer obligation framework (inferred from analogous jurisdiction structure — verify directly with WSCB Yukon).[^2][^3]
